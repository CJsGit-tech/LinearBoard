# W05 — The State of Web Design: 2016 Edition

**Source:** UXPin, copyright 2015; Ben Gremillion and Carrie Cousins (p. 6). Exact filename: `Web Design/The State Of Web Design - 2016 Edition.pdf`. PDF text pp. 1–60 read; originals pp. 1–2, 6, 9–60 inspected. Body folios match PDF pages, although p. 57 has the wrong running chapter header and the contents place Tiny Animations at 58 rather than its p. 57 start. A historical forecast with selected examples, not present-day standards or controlled user evidence. No depicted site or performance experiment was executed.

## Design the useful service and its changing states

Pelican/Penguin Reader connects chapter preview, purchase and in-browser reading; Netflix represents a watching service across individual titles (pp. 8–11). Retain considering what people do before and after a page, and choose a medium for their task. Do not conclude every site is a service of the same kind, people never seek a specific document, or UI quality is secondary to an abstract content-first declaration. The actual Pelican screenshot shows Read the first chapter and Buy the book actions, not proof of frictionless purchasing (p. 9).

Small-screen-first can reveal priorities without giving mobile users an impoverished task (pp. 14–17). The diagram shows primary/secondary content expanding across devices; The Guardian original includes headline imagery, navigation and a secondary story. Do not infer that a narrow viewport implies poor bandwidth, that this is always the easiest process, or that increased readership was caused by this redesign. Required content must remain reachable in relevant contexts.

Modular thinking connects labels/inputs, forms, sections and complete journeys over time (pp. 18–21). Keep the pattern's purpose, behavior and state transitions, inspect existing reusable components, and remove steps only when they are truly unnecessary. Atomic Design's five levels are one organizing method, not a required file/class hierarchy. The gray diagram illustrates a template and regions; moving a sidebar can affect sequence, emphasis and access even if its rectangle fits. Shared implementations are preferable to uncontrolled copy/paste drift; this is design-pro synthesis.

## Responsive decisions and technical corrections

The responsive/adaptive comparison presents continuous fluid layouts versus selected fixed layouts (pp. 22–25). Treat that as this book's simplified vocabulary, not a complete binary or a guarantee that either is easier, cheaper or better. Consider content constraints, legacy implementation, users and delivery scope. A dominant device class does not justify ignoring other supported widths, zoom or inputs; neither a breakpoint pair nor a few static screens verifies the gaps.

The physical/device/CSS-pixel explanation and its “six high-density pixels” figure are ambiguous (pp. 12–13). **Focused primary check 2026-10-25:** [MDN devicePixelRatio](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/devicePixelRatio) distinguishes physical from CSS-pixel resolution; page zoom can change that ratio. Density does not turn CSS layout pixels into physical screen dots or require choosing breakpoints by physical-pixel width. Choose assets and layout using the relevant coordinate system, not the source's terminology alone.

Flexbox diagrams show axes, alignment and reordered numbered units (pp. 26–30). Reordering is not permission to rearrange meaningful content “on a whim,” and horizontal direction depends on writing mode. **Focused primary check 2026-10-25:** [MDN ordering flex items](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Flexible_box_layout/Ordering_items) explains that order/reverse change visual presentation while source-based speech/tab order remains; correct source order must not be replaced with cosmetic reordering. Read these relevant sections, not every embedded example or full CSS specification. Check [meaningful sequence and focus order](../current-accessibility.md#meaningful-sequence). The 2015 browser matrix is historical and was not adopted as current compatibility evidence.

The image chapter substantially overlaps [W02's image discussion and primary corrections](W02.md#images-code-and-performance-need-verification) (pp. 31–35, 38–39). JPEG quality and compression are confused, PNG is incorrectly reduced to palette size, and the PNG chart repeats/misorders axis labels. A single image experiment does not establish a 70% cap or universal low-contrast/limited-palette requirement. Choose representations for meaningful content and compare actual output/bytes; CSS max-width changes display size, not necessarily downloaded bytes. “SVG uses lines” is a simplification, not a rule that any SVG is inherently small. Test resource and interaction costs across actual content pages; reducing another file does not excuse an unhelpful heavy hero. No benchmark or current third-party service recommendation follows from these originals.

## Evaluate trend options against the task

- **Media** (pp. 36–39): Sons of Gallipoli shows a play invitation, chapters/map/archive; Ford's cinematic image has a Skip control. Keep intentional viewing, skip/stop and useful content alternatives. Seamless HD is not mandatory and a toggle alone does not prove media accessibility. See [audio](../current-accessibility.md#audio-control) and [motion](../current-accessibility.md#moving-and-auto-updating-content).
- **Interaction** (pp. 40–42): Lightsaber Escape shows a phone pairing instruction; Slack's illustration is a marketing homepage. They do not establish that two-device play is a microinteraction, that all users want gamification, or that notifications/merchandise caused adoption. Feedback must serve a real task and preserve interruption control.
- **Navigation** (pp. 43–44): Mario Frigerio overlays named destinations on photography; Sweet Punk combines a project mosaic with a right-side navigation surface and close mark. A large menu may fit some sites but can conceal useful destinations. Keep understandable open/close, keyboard access and appropriate focus; splash/motion does not guarantee discoverability or deeper visits.
- **Grouping** (pp. 45–48): Under Armour's visual grid, Prime It's mixed job/story units, Dibi's yellow/pink illustration and Si le Soleil's asymmetric panels show different groupings. A card need not contain exactly one datum/action; splitting a screen does not automatically solve responsive reading order or create good spacing.
- **Personalization** (pp. 49–50): The North Face displays a jacket/activity question; Hulu's screenshot displays categories rather than verifying an individual's recommendation algorithm. The author's jacket anecdote is not a comparative result. Do not adopt invisible tracking as desirable by default; purposeful data, understandable suggestions and user control matter.
- **Type** (pp. 51–52): Tennent Brown combines very large angled words with a narrow body column; Samsung uses a prominent heading and small secondary controls. These static examples do not prove readability for all ages, establish a standard line length or guarantee that larger is always better. Edit for clarity and test actual font/content/zoom.
- **Scroll** (pp. 53–54): The 99 Properties of Power film image and Panache's graphic pattern illustrate dramatic composition. Reject the assertion that scrolling must entertain or users will leave. Avoid trapping navigation or requiring spectacle for ordinary reading.
- **Style/motion** (pp. 55–58): Ginventory's pale product presentation, Todoist's illustrated article, Portland's earthquake-preparedness scene and Dim Sum's image grid show varied forms. Flat/Material is not defined solely by the quantity of physical effects; no current platform requirement follows. Tiny animation is optional and must not hide required information or act as the sole progress/error signal.

The closing larger/bolder/app-like forecast and vendor trial (pp. 59–60) are historical statements. Do not use the claimed dissolution of web/native boundaries to erase runtime, keyboard or platform distinctions.

## Application

The Web game demo can connect world/gameplay/release/action as one journey while keeping direct access and useful content without autoplay. Mobile-web chat needs readable messages, a stable composer and truthful progress/retry across sizes. Shared components can preserve behavior while each demo uses an appropriate visual direction. These are design-pro applications, not measured outcomes from this book.
