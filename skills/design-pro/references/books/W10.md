# W10 — Web Design Is Dead

**Source:** Ben Hunt (image-only cover p. 1; closing signed Ben p. 46). Exact filename: `Web Design/Web Design is Dead.pdf`. Internal discussion places the work in 2015 and beyond (pp. 29, 32), but no separate publication-date verification was performed. PDF text pp. 1–46 read and all 46 original pages inspected. No printed page folios are visible; locators here are PDF pages. This is a historical professional/marketing argument, with autobiographical examples and promotion of the author's Open-Source Marketing project. It is not a usability experiment, contemporary labor-market survey, investment analysis or verified tool comparison.

## Define value before selecting production methods

Pp. 2–10 criticize producing a website merely because it is novel or technically possible. The useful question is what the intended users and organization need to accomplish; publishing capability alone does not demonstrate doing it well (p. 10). The original Yaxay forum screenshot dedicates substantial space to decorative chrome around a discussion table (p. 8), illustrating potential competition with content. That screenshot cannot prove task failure or establish that all decoration/creative identity is waste. P. 9 criticizes mandatory Enter Site splash pages and navigation requiring hover to reveal destinations: avoid unjustified access steps and undiscoverable essentials.

The clothing, printing-press and desktop-publishing analogies (pp. 3–5, 10–12) illustrate an argument about reusable production, not proof that the same market outcome must occur. The manuscript picture p. 4, WordArt chooser p. 11 and cluttered MySpace page p. 12 are historical examples. Do not adopt blanket claims about universal publishing access, all previous sites being ineffective, historical absence of spam/accessibility law, or a displaced worker necessarily being to blame for losing a home (pp. 2–3, 12). No legal/history verification was needed to use the design lesson, and these claims are not adopted as facts.

## Reuse is a candidate, not a quality guarantee

Pp. 11–22 describe CMSs, themes, platforms and specialized disciplines. The WordPress admin screenshot p. 14 displays an editor with title/body/post controls; it is historical, not today's implementation guidance. The skills maps pp. 7, 13, 15, 22, 27 grow blocks labelled production, graphics, CMS, usability, accessibility, mobile, SEO, PPC, social and video. They are conceptual illustrations with no measured scale, denominators or labor-market data. Heights/areas must not become quantitative estimates of effort, opportunity or importance. Accessibility is not a low-priority task merely because it appears low on the page.

The economy-of-scale diagram p. 20 cycles from accessible technology to more use, market opportunity and investment in improved products. It is a proposed reinforcing loop, not proof of guaranteed quality or an irreversible law. Morgan/Toyota photographs and unlike prices/model years (p. 17) are not matched durability tests. The author's old/new blog screenshots p. 18 differ in content and layout; they are not controlled evidence that a theme produced a faster, more usable site or a 13% overnight search lift (p. 19). The $49 versus estimated $30,000 comparison has unmatched scope and no lifecycle cost analysis. Do not repeat those as current costs, ROI, SEO outcomes or a reason to acquire a named product.

For design-pro, inspect existing components, tokens and dependencies; assess reuse against the specific workflow, content, quality and maintenance requirements. A reusable component may save effort but still needs integration, state, accessibility and runtime verification. Custom work is justified by a real unmet need, not restricted to affluent clients, nor automatically superior or obsolete (pp. 21–26). The wardrobe, catalog and furnished-room images (pp. 24–25) usefully distinguish designing a reusable part from composing parts into a coherent experience. They do not define the only three possible design careers.

## Context precedes tactics

Pp. 27–35 argue for accountable services and context-sensitive choices instead of prescribing a fashionable channel to every organization. Retain asking why a technique fits, what evidence supports that choice, and what would change the decision (pp. 30–32, 35). Reject the absolute claims that strategic skills are always easier, timeless common sense, impossible to standardize, immune to outsourcing, or that specialization guarantees security (pp. 26–31). These are professional opinions and forecasts rather than a current career prescription. A full-stack practitioner or small team can legitimately combine roles.

The four phases proposed on p. 34 are strategy, campaign design, delivery and live operation. They are useful responsibility distinctions, not a compulsory linear process or permission to expand a UI task into a whole marketing engagement. This book focuses on customer acquisition; many application tasks instead support learning, communication, internal work or other outcomes. Do not reduce every UX goal to selling or collecting prospects.

## Discovery questions without interview quotas

Pp. 35–38 ask about the offering organization, what is delivered, the promise, the important problem and the reachable audience. The named Circuit model (p. 36) is the author's framing, not an externally validated completeness theorem. Use its practical prompts for a public game page: who is it for, what experience is offered, what is actually promised, what does the audience need to decide, and what action matters? In mobile chat, clarify the conversation task and expected help rather than assuming all five marketing terms fit directly.

The source recommends dozens/hundreds of questions and describes a nineteen-page questionnaire (pp. 36–37). That is not design-pro's discovery contract. Ask the next consequential missing question, reuse answers and supplied materials, and stop when enough is known for the authorized deliverable. The source's advice to eliminate the client brief (p. 40) is rejected: collaborate on a shared brief that preserves user requirements, assumptions and unresolved decisions. A strategist's interpretation does not replace the user's authority or establish objective fact. Clarifying dependencies/ownership can help specialists avoid undoing one another (pp. 37–38), without requiring a new specialist per discipline or a large team. The historical ODesk search count p. 38 is not evidence of present demand or a statistically representative market.

## Journey design and evidence boundaries

Pp. 39–42 contrast different campaign choices for a trainer, equipment innovator, butcher and other organizations. These are author-reported cases. Claimed Facebook revenue increases, claims that a book was the only sufficient format, counterfactual wasted website spend and base-jumping equipment safety are not independently established here. Do not copy the revenue/safety claims into a product demo, treat innovative products as necessarily targeting early adopters, or recommend a channel on these stories alone.

The source separates awareness, targeting, capture, nurture, close and continuity (pp. 41–42). This is one possible acquisition journey, not six mandatory screens or a requirement to capture contact details whenever possible. A game visitor should be able to inspect gameplay, release and platform information without supplying data. A prototype's local action is not a real wishlist signup or purchase. Select steps around the authorized task and clearly distinguish optional subscription, interest and actual transaction outcomes.

The two questions on p. 42 are useful across UI patterns: in which circumstances is this technique appropriate, and how should it be used when selected? If several candidates remain plausible, define a discriminating check. Parallel marketing experiments are not automatically necessary, and this source does not provide an adequate statistical test design or universal conversion target.

## Delivery, learning and operation

Pp. 42–45 advocate coordinated delivery, testing assumptions and revising with new evidence rather than treating launch as the end. Keep explicit ownership, a working design contract and checks tied to the user's task. The preference for specialists and forecasts of dissolving agencies are conditional opinions (pp. 43–44). Do not treat a good initial strategy as sufficient for success or claim monitoring cannot reveal and correct a weak strategy; the source itself allows radical revisions (p. 45).

Separate assumptions from results and prototype simulation from production operation. Ongoing analytics, email, PR, SEO or optimization are suggestions in the source (pp. 44–45), not authorized integrations or scheduled monitoring for these demos. A bounded final deliverable and acceptance evidence are still needed despite p. 43's criticism of a fixation on final deliverables. Record actual limitations and an iteration route; do not invent post-launch measurements.

## Promotional material and scope

Pp. 32–34, 38, 42, 44–46 describe the author's project, free courses, future technique library and invitations to mailing lists/groups. Their current availability, licensing and completeness were not checked. The analogy between open contribution and successful software is a proposal, not proof that every contribution improves quality or permission to redistribute source books. No service was joined, product selected, message sent or course recommended. Financial/historical figures and names in the broader narrative (pp. 2, 7, 9, 14–19) were read as source claims and are not used as design evidence.

## Application and assessment

For the Web game page, document a concrete audience/action and show information needed to make that choice; judge whether release details, gameplay and the simulated action are discoverable and usable. For mobile-web chat, define conversation/navigation/recovery outcomes and check the implemented states rather than promising commercial results. These are design-pro applications of context-before-technique and iteration, not claims that this book validates either demo. Both modes can reuse existing capabilities while retaining a clear user brief and appropriate quality checks.
