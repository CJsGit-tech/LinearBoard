# W09 — Web Design Book of Trends 2015–2016

**Source:** UXPin, copyright 2015 (p. 3); author profiles Jerry Cao, Carrie Cousins and Kamil Zieba (p. 8). Exact filename: `Web Design/Web Design Book of Trends 2015-2016.pdf`. PDF text pp. 1–186 read and all 186 original pages inspected, including image-only covers and closing promotion. Printed body folios match PDF pages. The typography chapter's running headers incorrectly say Vibrant Colors (pp. 101–121); use the actual chapter/page, not that header, as the locator.

This is a historical, vendor-published selection of nine trends with screenshots, opinions, forecasts and tool lists. It substantially overlaps the library's flat/minimal/card/color/interaction books and W03/W07. Repetition is not independent corroboration. Examples describe the captured historical UI; no live site, animation, service integration or claimed user outcome was executed or independently measured.

## Flat design and usable signifiers

Pp. 9–27 distinguish strict flat styling from hybrids with selective depth, texture and photography. Original examples: HLK's statement and cyan About Us button versus Agency Survival Kit's photographed object (p. 11); Forest's phone/tree UI on a photographic background (p. 12); Duffy's product shadow/reflection and Helbak's product grid (p. 13); Intercom's communication diagram and signup control (p. 14). These illustrate stylistic combinations, not a historical timeline proven by matched tests. Smartphone photos on p. 10 depict different OS/device contexts, not a controlled usability comparison.

The useful decision is whether visual cues distinguish content, controls and hierarchy for the actual task (pp. 16–17). The book itself lists weakened signifiers among risks while elsewhere calling flat interfaces inherently intuitive (pp. 9, 25). Do not adopt the latter guarantee, or assume flat appearance means fast loading, automatic responsiveness or no hierarchy. One font, sans-serif, uniform strokes, large icons and ghost buttons are aesthetic choices, not requirements (pp. 14–21). P. 15's IndieGogo screenshot actually includes both filled and outlined pink actions, so it does not support a universal outline-only recipe. Munchery's instructional icons/Mixpanel's central illustration (p. 18), Beoplay/Arte/Rdio palettes (pp. 19–20), Dropbox's two guides (p. 20) and Miceli's sparse identity (p. 21) show different roles for content and styling. Do not infer learnability from visual simplicity alone.

The Material discussion (pp. 22–23) and Canal TP/Papertelevision/Emiliano galleries (pp. 24–25) are historical descriptions. More shadows or animation is not a definition of current Material guidance. Retain the possibility of purposeful depth without imposing a style purity rule. Resource lists pp. 26–27 were read as references, not adopted as current tools/free offers.

## Functional minimalism

Pp. 28–47 argue for removing distractions while keeping necessary content/navigation (p. 30). This is more useful than the contradictory claim that one remaining element guarantees communication (p. 29). Huncwot's four pathways and Overstock's shopping/navigation content (p. 29) serve different tasks; Edgar Schmitz's tiny contact treatment (p. 30) cannot establish equal comprehensibility. Quotes Magazine, Apple/Beats, ETQ and Outpost (pp. 31–34) illustrate editorial grouping, product imagery, concealed navigation and expressive type. A decorative headline style is not a prescription for body text.

Leen Heyne's jewelry/identity composition (p. 35) shows relative space around two focal subjects. Velvet Hammer's split, Squarespace's image/copy balance, Carlo Barberis's radial composition and Julie Flogeac's asymmetry (pp. 37–38) are possible arrangements, not four exhaustive or objectively safest solutions. We Ain't Plastic's sculptural gemstone and quiet labels (p. 39) illustrate a metaphor whose meaning still needs evaluation. Sparse luxury imagery does not guarantee attention, trust, sophistication, performance or usability.

Keep the distinction between simplifying around the task and imposing a sparse aesthetic (pp. 40–41). Reject age stereotypes about children/young adults being bored, the >44 hamburger threshold without contextual evidence, mandatory increasing density down the page, Z-shaped reading, one concept/visual per page, and automatic removal of footers/social information (pp. 33, 41–43). A black-and-white wireframe is an optional way to isolate structure, not a prerequisite. Mixd's product image, Words Can Save's story and Keecker's controls (pp. 45–46) do not justify compulsory animation. Tools pp. 46–47 are historical.

## Cards as coherent subjects

Pp. 48–63 define a card as a subject with related data, potentially including several fields/actions (p. 49). The UXPin library (p. 48), UGSmag/Erik Johansson (p. 50), Relevant (p. 51), Verge (p. 52), Dribbble (p. 53), Trello (p. 56), Contently (p. 57), historical Material collections (p. 58), Google Now/HOW arkitekter (p. 60) and Glass transit display (p. 61) demonstrate different structures. Pins, masonry and magazine previews overlap as categories; they are not an exhaustive taxonomy or proof of easy scanning. Contently's subject includes preview text and several metadata indicators; one concept does not mean one datum.

Retain spacing, meaningful crops, readable labels and consistent grouping (pp. 55–56). Do not add borders merely because the source says borders/padding; whitespace may suffice. Entire-card activation is suitable only when it has an unambiguous main action and does not conflict with secondary links/buttons, selection or nested controls. Touch screens do not turn cards into usable buttons with no extra implementation (p. 53). The p. 52 AirDrop narrative does not establish identical cross-device behavior or a sync contract. Trello's claimed superiority over lists and Contently's faster hiring are unsupported causal conclusions (pp. 57–58). Bigger cards, automatic updates and wearable-card inevitability are forecasts, not requirements (pp. 59–62). Prefer a table/list where comparison or sequential tasks demand it. Tool lists pp. 62–63 do not select dependencies for a new product.

## Goal-driven interaction

Pp. 64–79 offer five useful questions: user goal, functioning usability, signifiers, learnability and feedback (pp. 66–68). Apply these as inspection prompts rather than assuming every mailbox/hamburger symbol is understood. MixPlus's partial tape, downward arrow and choices (p. 67) illustrate cues; static images do not establish playlist creation success. BBQ Cultures' story tiles, Valentin's sparse greeting, Safety on the Slopes' route/content overlay, House of Borel's image sequence, Abbey Road's entry action, AIGA's timeline, Eventbrite's venue previews, Sonoran's Valley's start page, Mammut's adventure choices, Cinderella's promotional start and Blossom Type's custom wording (pp. 65, 68, 70–71, 73–77) serve distinct tasks. Eventbrite's p. 74 image is promotional venue selection, not proof of the seating editor's complete behavior.

The quoted interactivity research and 66% below-fold statistic are not enough here to establish universal persuasion or decide this project's layout (pp. 65–69). Do not hide initial purpose/release/action to force an investment in scrolling. Device-agnostic intent does not mean identical input. P. 71 includes passive notifications but p. 72 claims all microinteractions are user-initiated; explicitly model system triggers as well. Personalized user-created wording is distinct from tracking or geolocation (pp. 76–77); neither is automatically wanted. Wearable forecasts are historical, and no current native platform rule is established by tool links pp. 78–79.

## Motion, truthful feedback and control

Pp. 80–99 cover loading, navigation, hover, galleries, attention, scrolling, page motion and backgrounds. Retain an explicit purpose for motion and avoid competing movement (pp. 83–85, 93–94). The twelve film-animation principles on p. 82 are inspiration, not twelve compulsory interface effects. A 0.1-second activation heuristic and the vendor's 300ms transition example (p. 85) are not a universal duration budget; distinguish feedback onset, useful completion and transition length.

Reject the claim that animation is becoming mandatory (pp. 80, 95), deliberately making technology seem better than it is, and inserting an unnecessary loading sequence simply to entertain (pp. 85–87). A pending signal must describe actual operation. Brian Hoff's closed/open menu pair (pp. 87–88) supports the state relationship, but not tested smoothness, focus or keyboard access. Alectia's cards and UXPin's hovered download treatment (p. 89) show supplemental feedback, not permission to make hover the only sign of an action (pp. 88–90). Essential controls need a discoverable baseline and usable input alternatives.

Big Active's gallery arrows (p. 90), MY/STATIC/SELF's cube plus plain-text navigation (p. 91), For Better Coffee's illustrated process (p. 92), Hunger Crunch's scene/Play/Watch choices (p. 93), Terna's landscape (p. 94), dental feature blocks and Fleet Feet's product/cart controls (pp. 95–96), and Bolia's product composition (p. 97) have different informational roles. Nothing in these static pages proves which navigation users prefer, that motion is more valuable than coffee instructions, or that a cart animation confirms purchase. No motion was actually played. See [current motion and updating-content checks](../current-accessibility.md#moving-and-auto-updating-content); historical tools pp. 98–99 do not require jQuery or a new dependency.

## Typography that preserves meaning

Pp. 100–121 distinguish dramatic use from elaborate letterforms and discuss family, weight, scale, image overlays and custom lettering. Original gallery: Adidas campaign collage, Noveske landscape, Vimeo Cameo, White Frontier's editorial panels, Pack dog prompt and Spotify phone (pp. 100–105); Zero's heavy headline, Squarespace's serif title, Duffy's thin letters and Gogoro's pale heading (pp. 107–108); Adventure's script, Mikiya's object/label and Revelator's dark workflow (pp. 109–110); Under Armour's image annotation, Top Secret's quiet title, AIAIAI's product name, Reebok's overhead scene, Anja Rubik's photo-filled letters, Hotel Moscow's mixed lettering, Kraftwerk/Maaemo, RSQ, Vintage Hope, Send a Message's before/after, Orangina and Float (pp. 111–119).

These show expressive variation, not universally legible combinations. Reject >85pt as a general large-type threshold, tiny type compensated by whitespace or animation, white lettering guaranteed to contrast on images, uniform strokes as universally more readable, and browser rendering problems being a thing of the past (pp. 104–111). The book's own p. 113 priority is communicating meaning; p. 116's willingness to sacrifice legibility for aesthetics is not appropriate for essential task content. Match display type to short identity roles and assess actual language, loading/fallback, zoom/reflow and available space. This does not ban a custom family from readable body text merely because p. 115 says use it sparingly. Protect focal image subjects and text contrast across crops (p. 112); labels must not misidentify the photographed person (p. 113). Tool/service availability, inclusion and prices pp. 105, 120–121 remain historical.

## Color as one part of hierarchy

Pp. 122–138 use fashion/interior analogies and examples of monochrome, accents, blocking, textures, navigation and overlays. Versace/Cropp/G-Star (pp. 123–124) do not establish universal cross-industry tastes. The p. 124 higher-pixel-density-to-more-colors explanation is not adopted; [W05's focused primary technical clarification](W05.md#responsive-decisions-and-technical-corrections) separates device/CSS pixel concerns. The 216/98% discussion is historical and not a present audience statistic.

Linequality's green Muppet scene (p. 125), Hega's small red action (p. 126), Beoplay's repeated pink (p. 127), PFD's tinted tile (p. 128), Tacospillet/Joyride's textured imagery (pp. 129–130), Master & Dynamic/AIRism (p. 131), Reef's highlighted word and Mijlo's orange action (pp. 132–133), Coloured Lines' labeled rainbow menu/Frame's story (p. 134), Vimeo's sections, Zample's gradient, Piccsy's headline and Focus Lab/Namale overlays (pp. 135–137) offer stylistic options. A monochromatic palette does not eliminate clutter/contrast problems; color alone does not establish hierarchy or section meaning. Reinforce role with words, structure and state cues. Do not infer emotion, urgency, comprehension or conversion from hue, or assume a yellow filter makes a sad picture happy (p. 136). Tool lists pp. 137–138 are historical.

## Photography, content and provenance

Pp. 139–154 distinguish subject/context, hero placement, monochrome/tints and sourcing options. Jawbone shows its product in hands (p. 139); Nua/Blackhouse foreground product/food (p. 140); Cookiesound frames a road and story title (p. 141); Edwin's cropped torso/baseball context (p. 142), Goltz's desaturated interior/accent, Knuckles' workshop treatment (pp. 143–144), Woodbuds/Pure Fix/Anton and Irene (pp. 146–147), Kissmetrics/Microsoft/Squarespace (pp. 148–150), Portrait Machine's event images, Beth-Emily's staged shelf and Mod Squad's riders/type (pp. 151–153) illustrate differing messages. The original Edwin crop does not establish the model's age; do not repeat the prose's child claim as observed evidence.

Choose images to explain a real subject, use context or tone while preserving readable tasks. Full-screen placement, unusual angles, black-and-white and custom photography do not guarantee conversion or professionalism (pp. 141–146). Custom work's useful control/time/cost tradeoff is conditional; not all physical products require a commissioned shoot. The book's royalty-free/free-to-use and automatic company ownership statements (pp. 145, 147) do not establish an actual asset's permissions or ownership. Inspect the specific terms rather than adopting the generalization; this review is not a legal/license determination. Mixing/cropping assets can improve consistency but must not imply that stock depicts an actual product, customer, result or team when it does not (pp. 149–152). Do not present historical pricing, services, reverse-search completeness or the forecast that video replaces photography as established facts (pp. 151–154).

## Scroll structure and return paths

Pp. 155–169 mix finite narratives, infinite feeds and scroll-driven effects. Keep them separate in a brief. Jack Daniels/Apple Watch (pp. 155–156), Frame's story (p. 159), Make Your Money Matter's scene (p. 161), Jumo's two product sections (p. 162), Revelator's flow with click-ahead controls (p. 163), Bauer's product action, Grovemade's product section, Jumo's position dots (pp. 164–165), The Boat and Beatrate's play/gallery (pp. 166–167) illustrate a range of structures.

Retain clear progress/location, distinct navigation versus conversion actions, direct routes to known content and task-based testing (pp. 163–165, 168). Fixed menus and dots are possible aids, not compulsory or sufficient return paths. Do not require a long homepage with shorter subpages, make all sections viewport-sized, dismiss long text as inherently uninteresting, shrink everything instead of adapting, assume scrolling means engagement, or use the 66% statistic as a universal layout rule (pp. 157–165). Search/SEO claims, MacBook/Watch generalizations and pageless-future predictions are historical and unverified-current, not adopted (pp. 158, 160, 166, 168–169). Native crown behavior does not define a web interaction contract.

## Background assets and real constraints

Pp. 170–186 discuss images, video and animation, including image-only closing promotion p. 186. Wonderfully Wild/Big Cartel (pp. 170–171), Flipboard's signup over food (p. 172), Bose/Ashworth/Dandy's compositions (pp. 174–175), Airbnb's search overlay and Brindisa's booking scene (pp. 176–177), Fieldwork's illustration, ACN's lettering, Wagamama's illustration, Bellroy's snow/product, Fernando's blurred image/links and Color Plan's overlapping circles (pp. 178–183) are historical screenshots. They do not establish load speed, motion comfort, product durability or a completed signup/booking.

Retain purposeful crop selection, readable overlays, asset alternatives and resource-cost assessment (pp. 173–179). Do not use the >200ppi/72ppi, twice-as-many-pixels, DPI/PPI interchangeability or highest-resolution-PC descriptions as universal/current definitions (p. 171). The raster/vector distinction is useful but does not mandate SVG for every original graphic, guarantee performance at every size, or justify shipping the largest source file (pp. 172–173, 179). A good capture source can still need appropriate delivered variants.

No universal 1:1.5 camera ratio, 10–30-second video loop, mandatory background media or video-to-animation rule follows (pp. 176–180). Muting autoplay sound is insufficient by itself to address motion, task distraction and alternatives. Blur does not automatically reduce transferred bytes/loading time (p. 182); apparent 3D does not depend on an HD display (pp. 182–183). Use [current moving-content/control guidance](../current-accessibility.md#moving-and-auto-updating-content) and evaluate the actual implementation. Pp. 184–186 resource/promotion claims were read, not adopted as current availability or tool requirements.

## Application to the two modes

For the Web game page, combine purposeful game art, short identity type and clearly reachable release/gameplay/action information; a finite narrative can have direct anchors without forcing visitors through animation. For mobile-web chat, prioritize legible turns, usable composer/navigation and accurate response states; do not equate sending motion with server completion, require hover, hide controls to appear minimal or impose the same layout at every width. These are design-pro synthesis, not results of running the historical sites. Verify actual reading, interaction, focus, reflow, state recovery and media alternatives; no conversion or universal aesthetic outcome is promised.
