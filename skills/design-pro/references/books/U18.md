# U18 — Zen of White Space in Web UI Design: Balance, Contrast, Hierarchy

**Source:** UXPin, 2015; Jerry Cao and Jake Rocheleau (p. 7). Exact filename: `User Interface/Zen of White Space in Web UI Design - Balance, Contrast, Hierarchy.pdf`. All PDF text pp. 1–58 read, 54 relevant original pages inspected. PDF pages match body folios; exact images are in the separate coverage ledger. Historical pictured sites were not runtime-tested.

## Space expresses relationships

Negative space need not be white. Micro spacing within text/controls and macro separation between sections are useful scales; compositional, visual and textual are working descriptions rather than an exhaustive taxonomy (pp. 8–14). Inventory the actual content and intended relationship before adjusting gaps. A heading close to its first paragraph can signal ownership; more separation can mark a new thought (pp. 42–46). Too much separation can dissolve the intended relationship, a limit the book explicitly recognizes despite its “larger distance forces attention” heading (p. 27).

Functional purpose takes priority over decorative spaciousness (pp. 25, 41, 57). Neither perfect spatial values nor a compulsory sparse style follows. The Raphael Malka/Over Clothing/Themes Kingdom/Drew Wilson examples substantially repeat U16 (pp. 15–20); their repetition is not independent evidence. The 15-point attention budget is unsupported. Side-by-side grouping does not require CSS floats, and a single-page site need not fit one viewport.

## Hierarchy, contrast and action

Use space with size, weight, position, color and content to make a hierarchy understandable (pp. 21–41). Contrast is not defined solely by two pleasing complementary colors, as p. 23 suggests; the book itself broadens it to size, position and space on p. 36. “Twice as loud” is metaphorical, not a measurement. A claimed 10–20-second abandonment window is not a universal deadline (p. 24).

The alternating iMac/Wunderlist layouts illustrate possible arrangements; their screenshots do not establish mandatory Z-shaped gaze paths, forced attention, memory benefits or successful tasks (pp. 28–32). Do not make required navigation illegible simply to emphasize a hero. Asymmetry for its own sake and keeping users “on their toes” are not task goals.

Sketch's outlined trial and filled purchase controls show relative emphasis, not a measured conversion effect (pp. 33–35). Group related input and submit controls meaningfully. The book's Fitts argument concerns pointer movement assumptions, not keyboard completion time or a fixed target size. The Procreate example separates artwork, screenshots and feature content (pp. 36–37); lower-contrast body text still needs to be readable, and every section need not fill a screen.

Luxury, relaxed feeling and apparent ease are interpretations of the Marie Laurent/Grace Coote/Peeq examples, not measured usability or universal audience associations (pp. 38–40). Extra scrolling has a context-dependent cost even on a single-page site. Perceived attractiveness does not establish actual capability.

## Text and navigation limits

Make semantic and visual hierarchy coherent without imposing an H1–H4 maximum or making every heading require a unique font style (pp. 42–46). The suggested 3–5-foot/5-second blur check can reveal broad visual emphasis; it cannot certify reading, accessibility or task completion. Size still matters despite the p. 44 claim that it does not matter as much as relationships.

Lever's columns and stacked small-screen footer illustrate grouping; Geckoboard instead emphasizes selected destination links (pp. 47–49). The p. 48 prose describes closeness but calls it Uniform Connectedness; retain the observed proximity relationship without adopting that confused label. No visible “Footer” heading is inherently necessary; meaningful structure/names remain separate questions. Do not infer working reflow from two static screenshots.

Microsoft's dropdown/flyout and product/footer links illustrate different navigation densities (pp. 50–53). Check the actual activation area, keyboard path and focus/recovery, rather than accepting “perfect UX” from padding or hover behavior. Increasing gaps does not automatically solve choice overload. Different header/footer roles can justify different spacing; fitting links is not a reason to shrink essential text below usable size.

Separate line rhythm from paragraph separation (pp. 54–57). The suggested 1em text and 1.5–1.75em line height are starting heuristics, not universal standards or user override criteria. “Avoid line height larger than a typical line” is ambiguous beside that recommendation; do not confuse the complete line box with the gap. Relative units do not guarantee identical pixels across fonts, contexts and user settings. A paragraph boundary must remain discernible, without a mandatory gap larger than one line or a fixed physical reading distance.

## Application

For the game page, separate identity, gameplay and release/action information while keeping their relationships clear. For mobile chat, distinguish message paragraphs, turns and composer controls; preserve readable type and usable activation areas under reflow/keyboard conditions. These are design-pro applications, not outcomes demonstrated by the book.
