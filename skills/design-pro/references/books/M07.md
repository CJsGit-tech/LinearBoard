# M07 — Push Notification Best Practices

**Source:** Mobify, *Push Notification Best Practices: Helping You Push Through the Noise*. Original filename: `Mobile Design/Push Notification Best Practices.pdf`. All locators are PDF pages. No exact publication date or individual author is established in the text; the appendix cites 2014/2015-era material. This is a retail engagement vendor guide, not a neutral platform specification.

## Principles worth retaining

- **Choose the user need and channel first.** The urgency/tolerance exercise (p. 7) compares message urgency with recipients' tolerance. Use this to discuss whether an interruption helps a specific task, rather than assuming a push is better than email for every message. The chart illustrates a decision model, not a measured boundary.
- **Explain value before permission.** The opt-in sequence (p. 10) and onboarding guidance (pp. 18–19) defer asking until users can understand the benefit. Deliver what was promised afterward. Design-pro application: a release reminder can be optional and tied to a game the visitor actually follows; loading the marketing page alone is not a reason to request notifications.
- **Make the destination match the action.** The anatomy diagram separates image, title, detail and link (p. 12). Messaging guidance asks for a clear next step and a URL leading there (pp. 21–22). Verify the actual destination and state, not merely whether a click was recorded.
- **Distinguish message purposes.** The catalog contrasts promotional and non-promotional cases: price/wishlist alerts, replenishment, shipping, stock changes, weather and news (pp. 15–16). The useful unit is the event and its recipient benefit. The labels are commercially framed: stock scarcity and exclusives can still be promotional despite appearing in the non-promotional list.
- **Respect time and frequency.** Consider timezone and avoid interrupting sleep; start with useful messages instead of filling a quota (pp. 18, 22). The US evening-activity graph on p. 22 is historical aggregate data, not a universal send-time schedule. User settings, task urgency and local context should govern; these qualifications are design-pro synthesis.
- **Measure against the objective and watch fatigue.** The guide separates engagement and revenue measures and flags unsubscribes when duplicating content across channels (p. 24). Design-pro synthesis: opt-outs and complaints are relevant alongside clicks; use an appropriate comparison before attributing an outcome to the notification.

## Boundaries and disagreements

The source treats browsing history, demographic profiles and physical proximity as available segmentation inputs (pp. 4–6, 11, 20). Relevance does not itself authorize data collection or imply users want location tracking. Ask only for data needed for a user-understood purpose and provide usable refusal paths. This is design-pro judgment, not a legal conclusion derived from this book.

The guide encourages repeated pre-permission prompts after refusal (p. 19). Do not adopt repeated nagging or conceal the option to decline. Its fixed retry/permission descriptions for web and native apps are historical and must not be used as current browser/OS behavior. Likewise, background GPS availability, browser coverage and native/web reach differ by platform and permission; the source does not establish current support.

The recommendation to create urgency (p. 21) applies only when the underlying event actually expires. Do not invent scarcity. Its 60–120-character suggestion is a copywriting heuristic; actual truncation depends on content, script, device and presentation. Emoji are not a replacement for essential words or accessible meaning. These are design-pro qualifications.

## Evidence limitations

Statistics about opt-in, churn, immediate reading, conversion and order value (pp. 4–7, 11, 13, 24–25) are historical third-party or vendor claims. The appendix identifies sources (p. 27), but this review has not independently verified their methods. Opted-in and opted-out cohorts differ; higher retention in one group does not prove notification causality.

The p. 13 reach calculation compares a global internet-user pool multiplied by browser **revenue share** with downloads of one example retailer app. Those are different populations and measures, so the calculated reach is not a valid general comparison. The visible industry chart and calculation should not become design-pro benchmarks. A notification also is not guaranteed to be seen merely because permission was granted, despite the strong wording on p. 5.

Claims that every retailer needs push, that both channels are always best, or that deployment has negligible technical overhead (pp. 10, 24–25) are sales positions. The demos do not require a notification service, tracking integration or a native app merely to illustrate UI.
