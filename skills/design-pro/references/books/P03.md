# P03 — The Guide to Mockups

**Source:** UXPin; introduction signed Jerry Cao, co-written by Matt Ellis and Narek Khachatryan (PDF pp. 4–5). Exact filename: `Wireframes, Mockups, Prototypes/The Guide to Mockups.pdf`; 72 PDF pages, all text read and all original pages inspected. Folios match PDF pages. No explicit publication date established; iOS 8, Sketch 3 and a 2015 gallery URL place the examples in a historical context. Vendor promotions and links are source material, not current recommendations. No linked tutorials, live previews or authoring tools were executed.

## Role, fidelity and limits

Pp. 7–17 distinguish structural wireframes, usually static visual mockups, and interactive prototypes that can have low or high visual fidelity. Use those as communication roles, not a compulsory production sequence. P. 7 requires three preliminary stages, p. 15 allows jumping into a mockup, p. 26 allows a concept mockup to replace wireframing, and p. 64 again forbids skipping it. The contextual positions on pp. 26, 30–31, 63 and 69 are more useful: choose the least costly representation that resolves the actual uncertainty. Fidelity has several dimensions; more visual detail is not intrinsically higher design quality.

Original pp. 8–14 show historical Yelp examples, a newsletter mockup and a detailed prototype-editor view. The low-fi Yelp screenshot (p. 12) contains numbered review annotations; these are designer comments, not product notifications. The detailed version (p. 13) highlights Food with a dark tile and a red action strip. It retains the broad search/categories structure but adds photographic context, actions and styling; the book's same-pieces/same-places claim is approximate. A depicted selected state does not prove the item can be operated or that navigation works. This example and its explanation substantially overlap [U10](U10.md) and [P02](P02.md), rather than corroborating them independently.

Visual drafts can reveal clashes of hierarchy, color, type and spacing, and can support feasibility conversations (pp. 14–17). Designer immersion is not a substitute for actual participants. Present the problem, task, intended state and unanswered questions with the image (pp. 16–17). Reject the blanket exclusion of stakeholders from final decisions (pp. 17, 64); clarify decision ownership and resolve evidence/constraints collaboratively. A pleasing presentation cannot establish task success, implementation correctness or stakeholder comprehension.

## What to represent

Pp. 18–31 cover hierarchy, navigation, recurring patterns and detail. The sketch/grayscale/color Favorites sequence (p. 19) is a conceptual illustration. Include the content, control labels and relevant states needed for the decision. Original pp. 20 and 22 place text/navigation over large photographs; their appearance does not establish readable contrast across backgrounds or working routes. Use existing product tokens and components where applicable.

P. 21's F/Z viewing routes are not universal laws; use [U11's primary-source qualification](U11.md) and actual content/task context. Color mood and visual stimulation are not guaranteed comprehension or conversion. Menus, breadcrumbs, filters and links (pp. 22–23) are options with different purposes, not four mandatory or exhaustive navigation types. A flat app does not need breadcrumbs merely because entry bypasses its home. Social sharing is not a universal user goal (p. 24). A popular site's pattern is an example to inspect, not visual proof it solves this product's problem (pp. 24–25).

The conceptual time/accuracy curve p. 26 has no measured units or data; do not infer a quantitative cost law. Paper detail p. 27 can clarify image meaning, while an empty placeholder may suffice for a different question. The high/mid fidelity bundle examples pp. 28–30 differ in content, offer structure and emphasis, not just polish. Their price/countdown text is historical sample content; it neither authorizes artificial urgency nor proves a real offer. Reject universal claims that anyone understands high fidelity or that a 50ms impression gives a design deadline or an outcome forecast (pp. 29–30).

## Choose tools by constraints

Pp. 32–41 compare specialized tools, graphics tools and coded drafts. Pp. 38–40 explicitly present opposing practitioner arguments: early code may expose feasibility and support iteration, while separating visual exploration can reduce technical distraction. These are conditional tradeoffs, not measured productivity comparisons. Thirty minutes is a rhetorical example, not a benchmark. Non-final artifacts are not automatically waste; code also does not establish production readiness. HTML/CSS is suitable for a web experiment and does not replace native runtime validation. The claims that an all-in-one tool provides everything, graphics tools are only justified for highly visual work, or a realistic draft inevitably needs a graphics editor are too broad (pp. 33–41).

Historical Moqups/Balsamiq/UXPin feature limits, Photoshop/Sketch imports and paid/free tool claims are unverified-current. No product was installed, purchased or selected on that basis. Familiarity, actual supported behavior, existing workflow, revision cost and deliverable requirements matter more than a vendor's category.

## Maintainable visual assets and collaboration

Pp. 42–52 describe shape layers, non-destructive editing, type choices, grouped variants, reusable kits, naming/versioning and early technical discussion. Their screenshots show authoring operations, not a browser rendering test. A Photoshop Crisp setting does not guarantee identical rendering (p. 45). P. 46 conflates downloadable web fonts with universally installed web-safe fonts; retain a suitable fallback plan and actual text rather than images of body copy. Do not adopt the source's font exclusions, catalog sizes or subscription endorsement as requirements. Screen-reader access depends on the resulting semantics, not merely selecting browser text.

Reuse suitable source assets and retain editable originals; vector content can scale, but a Smart Object is not inherently vector or unlimited raster detail (pp. 48–49). A column grid can coordinate alignment without guaranteeing adaptation to all widths (pp. 50–51). Agree on meaningful file/layer names, revision identity and variant organization; do not assume only the content region changes. Ongoing implementation discussion (p. 52) is stronger than treating a final PSD as an executable specification.

Pp. 53–61 show Sketch 3 naming, outlined logotypes, shared type styles/symbols, an overview of multiple screens, a grid setup and CSS export/plugin menus. Preserve editable text when outlining a particular logo; outlining is not a body-copy accessibility technique. Shared styles/components can prevent divergence, while legitimate variants remain explicit. A zoomed-out board helps reveal missing routes but does not prove flow completeness. P. 57's 1170px/12-column/3px-gutter/95px-column values are a historical authoring preset, not a universal or verified framework layout specification. Exported CSS is a draft to reconcile with real tokens, behavior and responsive constraints (p. 58), not automatically production code. Popularity/price claims and plugin compatibility were not verified; none was installed.

## Process and a living style reference

Pp. 62–71 present alternatives: wireframe→mockup→development, or early low-fi interaction refined with detailed visuals. Choose according to uncertainty and available validation rather than always discarding or always requiring a separate prototype. A coded experiment may already be the relevant prototype. P. 64's simplicity advice concerns visual ornament; do not interpret its objection to high-contrast font styles as a request to reduce necessary foreground/background contrast. Reviewing competitors is a hypothesis source, not proof of their users' success.

Pp. 69–70 suggest capturing type, margins, palettes, brand choices and layout decisions while mockups evolve. The Yelp style screenshot p. 70 separates palette and background/border roles. **Synthesis:** extend the existing reusable token/component reference and record rationale, states and variants; screenshot fragments alone do not specify semantics, behavior or maintenance ownership. The game page and chatbot can document their shared styles and distinct platform behavior in companion notes without putting bibliographies in the interface. Pp. 71–72 close with collaborative iteration and vendor promotion, not independent tool or outcome evidence.
