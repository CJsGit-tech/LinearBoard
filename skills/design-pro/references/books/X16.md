# X16 — UX Gamification Redefined

**Source:** *UX Gamification Redefined: The Right & Wrong Strategies*, UXPin, copyright 2015 (pp. 1–3). Credits list Jerry Cao, Sergio Nouvel and Matt Ellis (p. 5). Original filename: `User Experience/UX Gamification Redefined.pdf`. Locators are PDF pages; body folios match. The cover on p. 1 is visual content, not an unreadable text page.

## Mechanism and purpose

The guide explores applying game-related mechanisms to otherwise useful activities, rather than simply adding points (pp. 6–11). The introduction adds a *Zombies, Run!* image (p. 6); KFC illustrates a promotional game (p. 8). The hook canvas on p. 10 connects internal/external triggers, action, variable reward and investment, with Pinterest as a worked interpretation. This is an explanatory framework, not proof that every habit or enjoyable activity follows exactly that cycle. Do not promise success from its claim that over 40% of time is habitual.

Most content overlaps the gamification chapter in [X14, pp. 116–134](X14.md#gamification-should-support-the-users-own-goal). The two editions are not independent evidence. This edition's page references remain useful for concept retrieval.

## Conditions, tradeoffs and exceptions

Arbitrary virtual currencies, badges, rankings and excessive congratulations can add cognitive work without improving the underlying task (pp. 12–17). The source's historical Foursquare/Swarm, enterprise penetration and job-training claims lack enough methods here to establish current effectiveness. Its claim that any extrinsic reward diminishes intrinsic motivation is too broad, and sits uneasily with its later praise of real product rewards.

Prefer progress people care about, truthful task state and opportunities to build useful skill (pp. 15–17). Let users define relevant milestones where practical. Progressive disclosure can avoid overwhelming beginners, but hiding necessary controls to create random rewards creates discovery problems. A helpful tutorial should not become mandatory repetitive hand-holding. Coaching may be the requested service, so the source's measurement-tool-versus-coach dichotomy is contextual.

Do not adopt the instruction to make users lose their sense of time. Successful task completion, user choice and the ability to stop are better assessment grounds than addiction or total time spent. Enjoyment and emotional investment alone do not establish a habit (p. 28). These are deliberate design-pro qualifications, not claims made by the guide.

## Seven historical examples

- **Duolingo (pp. 18–19):** structured practice and visible progression align with an existing desire to learn. Levels are activity/progression indicators; this guide does not establish fluency from scratch or a clinical/statistical learning-effect claim.
- **Sublime Text (pp. 19–21):** optional advanced capabilities can reward growing competence without interrupting basic use. A control buried in a menu is not automatically discoverable or self-explanatory. Provide relevant access/help rather than copying obscurity.
- **GitHub (pp. 21–22):** work and relationships can be made visible. Repository popularity and contribution counts do not rationally certify developer quality, completeness or deserved reputation; private work and different roles are absent from such measures.
- **Trello (pp. 22–23):** intermediate states and a visible done area distinguish ongoing from completed work. A binary checklist can still suit a simple task. Dragging needs appropriate alternatives; moving a card is not proof the work occurred.
- **Kiip (pp. 23–24):** external brand rewards illustrate incentives attached to ordinary activity. Real rewards can still be intrusive or distort motivation; they are not automatically meaningful or suitable for every app.
- **Nike+ (pp. 25–26):** self-set goals, progress and optional social comparison can support an already desired activity. Competition is not appropriate for everyone, nor is social support a universal prerequisite for every health goal. These examples do not establish health outcomes.
- **Reddit (pp. 26–27):** reputation scores can affect contribution and attention. Votes do not establish truth, expertise or social benefit; do not treat the source's points/privileges description as a current product specification.

## Application to both modes

Ask what benefit a repeated action supplies, whether the person wants that outcome, how progress is measured and how they can decline or stop. For the mobile chatbot, preserve conversation progress without streaks, unnecessary badges or pressured return prompts. A game marketing page can express a game's identity without gamifying a visitor's navigation. Neither demo needs an incentive system merely to reference this book. Pages 28–29 conclude and promote the vendor.
