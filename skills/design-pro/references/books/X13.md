# X13 — Timeless UX Design Trends: Web UX Design

**Source:** UXPin, copyright 2015 (p. 3). Original filename: `User Experience/Timeless UX Design Trends - Web UX Design.pdf`. No individual author is identified in this edition's front matter. Locators are PDF pages; body folios match. Pages 1–2 are covers, not unreadable missing text. Historical examples overlap substantially with the opening chapter of [X14](X14.md).

## Design the service and its paths

An attractive page can still fail the person's task (pp. 5–11). Relate layout and controls to the experience they enable. Playlist reordering illustrates user control (p. 10), but drag-and-drop needs appropriate non-drag alternatives. The source's definition of UI as what can be seen/touched omits nonvisual interfaces; UX is also more than subjective delight.

Reusable templates and patterns can free time for task/context decisions (pp. 12–16). A multistep flight-booking form may reduce complexity; it does not establish that every donation form must have one step. The claims that websites are mandatory, mobile-first always applies, or machines cannot learn UX are 2015 positions, not verified current facts. The book's service emphasis remains useful without accepting its predictions about industries or AI.

Consider the complete activity, including other devices, services and offline touchpoints (pp. 17–20). The concert example joins discovery, ticket purchase, planning and transport. A simple directions interaction can be self-contained from the person's perspective without being technically independent of external services. Coordinating design, content and implementation prevents local optimizations from breaking the whole task. “Waterfalls are obsolete” is rhetoric around an acknowledged oversimplification, not evidence that every sequential process fails.

## Collaboration and discovery

Invite relevant perspectives early, explore alternatives, then clarify decision responsibilities while staying open to useful feedback (pp. 21–23). The author's exclusion of marketers from implementation decisions is contextual, not a restriction based on job title. Sales/support can expose recurring problems, but their agreement is not validation by representative users. Bring developers into feasibility decisions before final handoff. The T-shaped diagram combines deep expertise with cross-disciplinary understanding (p. 22), not a requirement that everyone master every role.

Ask about actual pains, workarounds and where devices enter the task (pp. 24–26). Group research findings into useful patterns, then connect stories, scenarios and journeys. Five interviews is an author heuristic, not guaranteed sufficiency. The p. 25 spectrum graphic puts preferences such as Illustrator/Sketch and Star Wars/Star Trek on continuous axes; nominal categories are not inherently measurable spectra. Do not generate fictional psychographics or numerical clusters from that example. The scenario on p. 26 supplies time pressure and a content mismatch that change the design problem; label invented scenarios as assumptions.

## Interface quality is part of task quality

Check consistent labels, content/visual tone, information architecture and accessibility together (p. 27). Accessibility is not optional merely because the source calls it a possible nice-to-have. Its claims about search ranking and reduced maintenance are not established by this book; use [current accessibility guidance](../current-accessibility.md) for applicable requirements.

Map useful paths before polishing individual pages and provide understandable feedback for actions (pp. 28–30). Prompt feedback and familiar patterns can reduce uncertainty. The suggested 0.1-second response is a heuristic, not a requirement that every task finish instantly. Humor, bouncing hearts and animation are optional; choose them for context and respect reduced motion. Fewer steps alone does not establish less effort, better comprehension or appropriate safeguards.

Salience can clarify importance, but pricing anchors and loss aversion do not justify deception (p. 30). Never invent a limited offer or pressure the user with false urgency. This qualification matters for the game-release demo's primary action and release information.

## Prototype to learn, without overstating proof

Early rough prototypes can reveal structural/task problems before substantial visual investment; later fidelity can answer different questions (pp. 30–31). Testing and revision reduce attachment to a first idea. The source overstates prototyping as the only sure way to gauge success: no prototype guarantees success, and production outcomes need different evidence. Five users, a fixed fidelity ladder and recording every session are not universal requirements. Choose relevant participants and methods; record only when appropriate and authorized. A local browser check is not such a user study.

## Historical cases and limits

- **Vine (pp. 32–34):** the source illustrates continuity of subscriptions across desktop/mobile and a readable card feed. It describes seven-second loops; that number is not needed for the design principle and is not adopted as a verified product specification. Claims of seamless transfer require behavior checks, not screenshots. Short attention-span and market-monopoly rhetoric is unsupported here.
- **VW (pp. 35–36):** configuration connects to finding an available vehicle/dealer, illustrating how a visual control can support an end-to-end offline outcome. The screenshots do not verify live inventory matching or sales impact.
- **Virgin America (pp. 36–37):** responsive booking and attention to travelers' tasks illustrate adaptation. Awards, first-in-industry and customer claims are historical reports, not independently verified performance evidence.
- **Pelican Books (pp. 37–38):** reflowable reading content can serve preview tasks better than an image scan on a small screen. “A thousand times easier” is rhetoric, not a measured result; the stated sales benefit is an inference.

The ecosystem conclusion supports coordination and coherent journeys (p. 39); it does not mean every change affects every component equally or that solo work cannot succeed. Page 40 is vendor promotion. For both design modes, preserve recognizable tasks and meaningful context across surfaces while adapting interaction and presentation to each platform.
