# T02 — Dramatic Typography

**Source:** UXPin, *Web UI Trends Present & Future: Dramatic Typography*, copyright 2015; Carrie Cousins, Jerry Cao and a contributor identified as Krzysztof on p. 4. Original filename: `Typography/Web UI Trends Present & Future - Dramatic Typography.pdf`. Locators are PDF pages.

## Practical techniques

- **Drama can come from composition, not an exotic font.** Size, weight, placement and a limited family can create emphasis (pp. 5–8). Readability and the letterforms' actual strokes still matter.
- **Use size contrast deliberately.** Oversized text establishes a dominant message (pp. 11–13); small text in substantial open space creates a different relationship to imagery (pp. 14–15). Neither is a reason to make essential release information too small to read.
- **Check text and imagery as one composition.** Evaluate their contrast at different breakpoints; keep text off important faces, products or identifying details (pp. 16–17). The words and image should communicate compatible meanings rather than competing identities.
- **Constrain a complex effect.** Image-filled letters, flourishes and custom lettering work best when the surrounding composition gives them space (pp. 17–20). Choose a technique that supports the brand rather than combining every visual treatment (pp. 23–24).
- **Distinguish decorative letterforms from necessary words.** Decide whether the message must be comprehensible or is an optional visual discovery (pp. 21–22). Required instructions, navigation and release details cannot rely on unreadable art. The last qualification is design-pro synthesis.

## Applications and verification

For the game page, explore a distinctive title paired with a legible supporting type system. Check the title's wrapping and its relationship to the primary action across viewport widths. Keep release date and platform information as readable text, even if a separate decorative wordmark is used. These are synthesized applications of pp. 11–17 and 21–24.

For a chatbot, expressive branding may belong in the entry screen; long message bodies should prioritize reading over display effects. A display treatment that succeeds in a short heading does not establish suitability for a transcript.

## Exceptions and evidence limits

The source's “often bigger than 85 points” description (p. 12) records a trend, not a minimum. Recommendations to make lettering uncomfortably large then reduce it (p. 13), or emphasize tiny type with animation (p. 15), need testing against the real content and users. Do not use animation as compensation for illegibility.

2015 trend claims, font popularity, service availability/prices and compatibility statements (pp. 9–10, 20, 25–28) are historical. Essential text rendered into an image raises separate accessibility and adaptation questions; the source's mention of image embedding on p. 20 is not a universal implementation recommendation. Current contrast and reflow requirements are summarized separately in [current accessibility sources](../current-accessibility.md).
