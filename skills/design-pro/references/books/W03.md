# W03 — The Best Web Designs of 2015–2016

**Source:** UXPin, 2015; cover subtitle *The Trendsetter's Ultimate Lookbook*; Ben Gremillion, Carrie Cousins, Jerry Cao and Matt Ellis (original p. 4 order). Exact filename: `Web Design/The Best Web Designs of 2015-2016.pdf`. PDF text pp. 1–157 read; originals pp. 1–2, 4–157 inspected. Body folios match PDF pages. A curated historical gallery with vendor tutorials, not a comparative usability study or current trend forecast. Screenshots were inspected; the depicted sites, animations, tools and commercial outcomes were not executed or verified.

## Interaction should earn its place

The first gallery presents nine main and nine additional sites (pp. 7–23). Keep meaningful feedback, optional exploration and a clear relationship between interaction and content. Do not infer that time invested makes a site enjoyable: the self-justification argument supplies no task evidence (p. 8). A longer session can also indicate difficulty.

KFC uses character choices and a time sequence (pp. 8–10); Airbnb pairs scene imagery with a trip search (p. 10); Chanel layers poetic copy and photography (p. 13). These show different roles for imagery and interaction, not measured emotional learning or booking effects. Lærepenger's name/demographic fields and cursor-circle choices do not justify collecting age, sex or location for unrelated tasks or assuming small forms never deter participation (pp. 11–12). Keep collection purposeful and provide accessible input alternatives.

Space Needle's upward journey matches its subject, while Nest 100's constellation and hover exploration match an optional anniversary exhibit (pp. 13–15). Neither licenses unusual scrolling or hidden required actions for routine work. Beatbox visibly labels drum keyboard controls and kit changes (p. 16); Aquatilis combines specimen imagery, labels and part thumbnails (p. 17). Inspect actual input access rather than inferring it from labels. The YouTube quiz's drag-and-drop and dancing load mask are optional effects, not reasons to require dragging or conceal genuine loading state (pp. 17–18).

Additional originals include Cinderella's layered scene, Happy Forecast's floating map, Mammut's mountain panorama, Make Your Money Matter's illustrated bank, House of Borel's panels, Abbey Road's entrance, Bugaboo's color selection, Beoplay's audio invitation and Coin's product story (pp. 19–23). Their screenshots establish composition, not task completion. The button expansion/collapse tutorial (pp. 24–26) can illustrate state transitions; its editor controls and historical animation workaround are not implementation requirements. Preserve meaning, focus and usable return paths.

## Minimal appearance and cards are choices

The minimalist gallery retains content hierarchy, space and restraint while showing substantial variation (pp. 27–44). Apple, Bruno Triplet, ETQ, Callens, Derwent, Outpost, Leen Heyne, Velvet Hammer, We Ain't Plastic, Carlo Barberis, Squarespace, Mikiya Kobayashi, Nua Bikes, Elite, Château d'Yquem, Bulgari, RESPECT BRO, Maaemo, Ava, WXG, Facebook Paper, Symbolset, Lapka and NTN include photography, oversized marks, thin type, unusual navigation and product detail (pp. 29–42). Do not turn this selection into rules that sparse pages are automatically faster, easier to maintain or responsive, that a hamburger is always best, or that one concept must occupy one screen. National/luxury stereotypes are not design requirements. Primitive shapes and icon libraries alone do not create named, operable controls (pp. 43–44).

A card is a coherent subject that may contain several related fields and actions; the Google knowledge-card original demonstrates that directly (p. 45). Card shapes do not solve choice overload or create responsiveness by themselves. The gallery includes Google Now, Pinterest, Instagram, InkPeek, day-after-day, NYTimes, ISSUU, Polyvore, HOW, Hornig, Etsy, Formerly Yes and thirteen additional examples (pp. 47–68). Relevant observed differences:

- Pinterest changes visible controls on hover; Instagram combines a full-width image with action icons and comments; InkPeek shows titles and a detail surface over the grid (pp. 48–51). The latter screenshot does not alone establish the new-page routing asserted in prose. Keep required actions available to non-hover inputs.
- ISSUU uses a Read Now detail overlay; Etsy includes product and seller/collection composites; Formerly Yes opens bowl details with close and Add to bag controls (pp. 54–61). Do not infer increased sales, low latency or correct focus handling from the static states.
- YouTube/Contently, Relevant/Dribbble, Jetsetter/UXPin, Vox/Upworthy, Digg/Netflix, Behance/PatternTap and Bezar illustrate different densities and kinds of content (pp. 62–68). Text-only units are possible; image-first is not universal.

The card tutorial builds with actual content, repeats units, varies lengths and explores a masonry layout (pp. 69–72). Retain testing content variation. Its 200px square, three samples and tool steps are examples, not target sizes or proof of scalability. Use an aligned list/table when comparing shared attributes matters more than independent visual discovery.

## Flat styling, typography and color require task checks

Flat 2.0 is an informal historical style, substantially overlapping U04/U14 (pp. 73–92). The originals move from drawn objects and shadows through full illustrations, photos and product scenes: Cybeer Bar/Christmas Joy, Dropbox/450 GSM, Intercom/Agency Survival Kit, Hello Baby/Helbak, Paye Ton Caps/CSSConf, TakeIt/Santa Tracker, For Better Coffee/Two Dots, Quotes/Bear, Stash/Acapo, Liberio/Kaipoche, Treehouse/Sceatt, Evolve Wealth/Black Tomato and Lander (pp. 73–87). Studio Neat, Nebia, Virgin America, Uber, Lix and Headspace show hybrids (pp. 88–90). These choices do not mandate large saturated surfaces, guarantee friendliness, or verify pictured product claims. Controls must remain recognizable; the vendor libraries/primitives pp. 91–92 are historical offerings.

Typography can express identity and establish reading order while preserving legibility (pp. 93–110). Nike's lace lettering, Packdog's large question, Wikipedia's multilingual portal/article and Mikiya's small but spaced brand differ from Anja Rubik's image-filled letters, Maaemo's custom mark, Vintage Hope's rough display and Float's script (pp. 93–100). Pop-Up Magazine's print metaphor and The Nation's article hierarchy illustrate distinct editorial roles (p. 101). Royal Drawing School and Mister French use varied styles; Cienne and La Femme Fatale emphasize imagery; Sassi Holford uses light text; Tomas Bata uses an unusual title; Thankful layers thin lettering over a scene; Aquatilis contrasts loud headings with quieter copy (pp. 102–105). Two Arms' slant suggests running, while Drexel builds a stepwise text hierarchy (p. 106); Fight 4 Pride, Slack, Master Dynamic, Aquatilis and Elevate complete the gallery (pp. 107–109).

Do not adopt definitions of serif/sans as exhaustive, simplistic color-emotion guarantees, “vision is dominant” as a universal user model, or visual appearance as equally important as meaning in every task. The chapter says 21 examples despite a 25-site heading and twenty main/five additional entries. Counted examples are not independent experiments. Decorative brand lettering may be appropriate without being suitable for body text; light-on-photo examples still need actual contrast checks. The font-panel tutorial is a historical tool example, not a requirement to use its advertised font collection (p. 110).

Color can create grouping and emphasis; choose a restrained accent or a broader palette for a reason (pp. 111–133). Originals show Dragone's blue scene, Tumblr's colored sections, Bose's angled product bands, Joyride's warm header, Line Equality's lime, Mijlo's small orange accent, Zample's gradients, Crop Trust's metric tiles, Focus Lab's photo overlay, 10andThree's photography, Apps' product scenes, Frankensim's simulated windows, Red Bull's editorial panels, TWO5SIX's lettering, Solo's colored sections and The Verge's gradient cards (pp. 111–126). De Broc, Fitbit, splitsecnd, Shaker, Murakami, Neemble, Nest and Evernote add different accent/background balances (pp. 127–130). This overlaps U04/U15. Do not copy pictured agricultural statistics, product safety claims or prices as verified facts.

The p. 111 explanation confuses pixel density with color capability; monochrome does not eliminate attention competition or ensure contrast. Color-only section identification is insufficient. Neither saturation nor a gradient guarantees fun, comprehension or conversion. The final prediction is historical, and the palette picker/saved-color controls pp. 132–133 do not replace project tokens or accessible state checks.

## Separate scrolling formats

A finite narrative page, an automatically extended feed and scroll-controlled animation are different interaction choices (pp. 134–157). Facebook/Twitter illustrate feeds; Heart Kids combines sticky access and a story; The Boat presents an illustrated narrative; MCA's helmets supply section markers (pp. 134–139). The infographic's neuroscience/learning percentages are not adopted as established evidence (pp. 140–141). Junko's Story pairs restrained text with artifacts; this is an observed editorial composition, not independently verified historical research (pp. 141–142).

Beagle connects feature scenes through a paper metaphor, Bowhead shows bag features, The Kings Arms uses simpler photographic sections, Wall's/Airpure adds left navigation and a progress bar, Beatrate includes video/carousel, and Space Needle repeats its upward metaphor (pp. 143–148). Keep, Lexus, One John St, One to See One to Kill, Cameo, Squarespace, Yep, Beoplay, Inbox and Reebok round out the gallery (pp. 149–153). Screenshots cannot establish enjoyable motion, accessible autoplay or comprehension.

Longer narrative can suit explanation, while direct anchors, meaningful sections and preserved location can support finding known information. Do not require every section to fill a viewport, infer length solely from width, force unusual scroll direction or assert sticky navigation prevents all disorientation. Motion is not more important than content. Address resource cost, loss of orientation and recovery; the source itself flags these risks (p. 154). Design-pro synthesis adds preserving focus/history position, essential content without decorative animation and user-controlled media; consult [current accessibility references](../current-accessibility.md) for applicable criteria.

The show-on-scroll tutorial sets an element at 500px, a 300px trigger and a 200px lead, then toggles hidden content and adds a lower dummy element (pp. 154–156). These are tool-specific example coordinates, not a universal offset. Do not hide essential content until an animation fires, toggle away a person's reading target, or add meaningless content just to manufacture scroll. No tutorial was executed here; p. 157 is vendor promotion.

## Application

For the game release page, use a visual world and a concise gameplay sequence alongside directly reachable release/platform details and the primary action. For mobile-web chat, readable turns, meaningful navigation and honest progress take priority over gallery effects. These are design-pro applications, not tested outcomes from this source.
