# U12 — Web UI Trends Present & Future: Card-Based Design Patterns

**Source:** UXPin, 2015; Carrie Cousins and Jerry Cao credited p. 5. Exact filename: `User Interface/Web UI Trends Present & Future - Card-Based Design Patterns.pdf`. All text pp. 1–31 read; 28 relevant original pages inspected. Citations use PDF pages, matching visible folios. Original screenshots establish composition and visible controls, not current product capabilities or tested usability.

## A coherent unit, not a compulsory shape

The useful premise is a card as a coherent subject/task grouping, possibly containing image, title, metadata and actions (pp. 6–8). Its primary action should be understandable. “One piece of information” does not literally mean one datum: the book's examples combine multiple related fields (pp. 22–24). Nor does every visual group need to be clickable or launch detail. P. 27 itself acknowledges cards that directly present information.

The Guardian's horizontal topic containers and priority ordering describe one publisher's organization, not a universal definition of all containers (pp. 8–9). Distinguish pin-style collections, tiles, magazine sections, regular grids and masonry rather than equating their reading order and task fit (pp. 9–12). Familiarity/professionalism, universal rectangular-phone fit, and improved trust/usability do not follow automatically from a card shape. The dates/origin/terminology of Metro and predictions of fashion are historical claims, not a current design-system prescription.

## Organization and responsive behavior require actual decisions

Card sorting can explore how people group or label content; a paper card in that research method does not imply a card-shaped visual interface (p. 13). A designer sorting alone is an initial hypothesis, not user validation. The book's “at least five” is not a justified universal sample size or proof of the most usable structure. Grouping results do not replace navigation/task evaluation, even if the final view is an infinite feed.

Cards can adapt related content to different widths, but reflow, text expansion, reading order, focus and input need to be implemented and checked (pp. 14–20). A visually reordered masonry layout may create a different semantic route; preserve meaning, not just equal gutters. A phone-shaped rectangle is neither proof of responsiveness nor evidence of native execution. The historical AirDrop accept/decline description does not establish today's platform behavior. No current native requirement is derived from it.

The pros/cons recognize aggregation, independent summaries and visual overload (pp. 17–20). Equal emphasis may suit some browsing but not a task requiring priorities or aligned comparison. The later mix of oversized and small cards explicitly introduces hierarchy (pp. 26–27), qualifying the earlier equal-hierarchy discussion. A magazine layout does not automatically solve clutter, and the book provides no comparison proving imperfect cards perform worse than all linear layouts.

## Retained practices and limits

Give each unit a clear purpose; group related elements using intentional space; crop useful images; make type readable and controls usable (pp. 21–24). Choose hierarchy and density by content/task. Check the whole collection as well as an individual card, since scanning, order and repetition matter.

Reject universal image occupancy of 50–75%, fixed small-card dimensions, mandatory sans serif, a required unexpected animation, or hover-only sharing (pp. 19, 22–23). A unit may need no image or sharing at all. The book's Fitts reference does not establish one numerical gap or target standard. Current target and keyboard checks remain separate. Effects should serve understanding/state rather than novelty. Adding photos or a background does not establish findability, readability or user preference.

The future chapter references historical Material Design paper metaphors, live tiles and wearable cards (pp. 25–28). None mandates Material styling, automatic refresh, video or a wearable integration. Existing project components take priority. The final resource list's libraries, free offers and availability were not checked as current recommendations (pp. 29–31). Card presentation does not justify disguising paid content as ordinary material.

## Original-gallery observations

- Citia's “card-powered” marketing panel and UXPin ebook covers (pp. 6–7) show distinct use cases, not independent outcome evidence. The Guardian tablet/laptop photograph and news layout (pp. 8–9) illustrate a responsive claim and visible priority groups; the image cannot prove actual reflow.
- UGSMAG's media cards, Windows tiles, Erik Johansson's mixed-size photographs and Relevant Magazine's articles/secondary rail (pp. 9–12) have different content and navigation. Several include promotion; do not infer all cards must carry ads.
- P. 13 depicts physical sorting cards. Google Now's sample music/film/news units and The Verge's unequal colored story blocks (p. 15) show why card hierarchy need not be uniform. No ranking or live updates were tested.
- Bezar's product imagery and Realtor's aligned property rows (p. 17) differ materially in visual comparison; the real-estate screenshot also contains a sponsored listing/banner. Dribbble p. 18 shows a regular gallery with small metadata, not a requirement for every product.
- Elite's photographic portfolio (p. 21), Trello's board (p. 23) and Contently's article portfolio (p. 24) demonstrate that collection, workflow and article browsing require different actions. Static controls do not prove drag/reorder or sharing behavior.
- Historical Android travel/music examples p. 25, HOW arkitekter's large colored groups p. 26, and the Glass route/time panel p. 27 show different density and interaction needs. The route panel includes text as well as colored circles; do not encode line/status solely in color. The back cover p. 31 is vendor promotion, not capability verification.

## Application

A game-page feature summary can use a card only if the grouping helps explain a distinct gameplay idea. A chatbot's coherent turn need not become a stack of bordered cards; retain readable message flow and visible recovery. These are design-pro applications, not observed outcomes. Much of the topic overlaps M03 and other library examples and is not independent corroboration merely because another PDF repeats it.
