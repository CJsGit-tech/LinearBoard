# U13 — Web UI Trends Present & Future: The Elegance of Minimalism

**Source:** UXPin, 2015; Jerry Cao and Carrie Cousins credited p. 5. Exact filename: `User Interface/Web UI Trends Present & Future - The Elegance of Minimalism.pdf`. Citations use PDF pages, matching visible folios. All text pp. 1–36 read and 33 relevant original pages inspected. Exact image pages are in the separate coverage ledger. No pictured site's functionality, performance or accessibility was tested.

## Remove unnecessary competition while preserving the task

The book frames minimalism as intentional emphasis on useful content, not merely empty white screens (pp. 6–8). It distinguishes a philosophy from a single flat, grayscale, symmetrical or text-only aesthetic (pp. 9–18, 22–23, 29–33). Keep that distinction when adapting an existing product: the task, clear controls and useful information matter more than attaining a stylistic label.

Negative space can exist on a colored surface, and alignment need not mean centering (pp. 13–18). Grids, scale and relative placement can organize content. The author's horizontal/approximate/radial/asymmetric categories are illustrative composition vocabulary, not an exhaustive taxonomy or fixed gaze route. Claimed luxury, sophistication, comfort and timeless appeal are interpretations, not demonstrated effects on every viewer.

## Discoverability and readability survive subtraction

The navigation discussion acknowledges that collapsing options can reduce discoverability and asks for actual primary/secondary functions and context (pp. 11–12). Retain that tradeoff; do not choose an unlabeled icon merely to reduce the number of visible elements. The over-44 age cutoff is a secondary historical claim with insufficient context here for a universal rule.

Contrast in size, weight, color or placement can express hierarchy, but must not make required information unreadable (pp. 19–20). The source makes an exception for tiny body copy because the art director's visual work is the focal point. Design-pro does not adopt that exception as permission to make body content inaccessible: visual secondary importance does not remove the need to read it. A pale screenshot likewise cannot establish adequate contrast.

The p. 24 prescriptions of at most five sections/top-five items, one concept per page, black-and-white-first and one destination/CTA are not universal requirements. The book's appeal to Hick's Law does not establish that every page may have only one route. Do not delete useful search, contact, recovery, alternative paths or navigation for a count. The later warning explicitly recognizes icon-only confusion and unnecessarily linear journeys (pp. 32–33), qualifying the earlier aggressive subtraction.

## Content complexity, audience and performance

The book identifies difficulties combining a sparse aesthetic with dense content, advertisements and many functions (pp. 21–27). Resolve actual priorities and information architecture; neither a game page, a large catalog nor content for children is automatically excluded from a clear restrained design. The claim that young users find minimalism boring or have uniformly short attention spans is unsupported as a decision rule. Street-fashion audiences do not automatically understand hover-only hidden product details (pp. 26–27).

Concise overview plus useful depth can suit a public page (pp. 22–23), but increasing density monotonically down the scroll or alternating every section into a Z is not obligatory. A clear entry page should not become an extra barrier to necessary content. Content inventory and user goals determine what must remain.

Fewer visual elements do not alone prove faster loading; a sparse-looking page can still deliver large images, fonts, video or scripts (p. 28). Measure the actual implementation and environment. Longevity, future texture/color/animation trends, and historical resource/trial availability are not present-day standards (pp. 28–36). No new current-market recommendation is inferred from them.

## Original visual examples

- Paprika's portfolio grid, Minerva's sparse intro and Fabrik's bold product/logo photograph show different balances of content and branding (pp. 7–8). They do not prove a uniform neutral palette or layout.
- Wonderful Colorado's circular illustrated scene, makgoods' hero with Shop/Watch actions, Thru You Too's oversized lettering and Hibou's heavy headline combine different imagery/type/navigation (pp. 9–12). The hero has more than one action; it need not become a universal single-action template.
- Lindvall's aqua introductory page and white interior collection illustrate colored negative space but also very pale text/linework at this reproduction scale (pp. 13–14). Do not copy their colors without checking the actual rendering.
- Arko mixes a large image with columns; Huncwot presents evenly spaced service groups; Squarespace balances screen image/copy; Carlo Barberis centers its visual; Julie Flogeac juxtaposes portrait/content regions (pp. 16–18). These are compositions, not gaze measurements.
- Jorge Riera's large graphic on the home page and oversized name beside tiny biography illustrate extreme typographic contrast (pp. 19–20). The small biography is not exempt from readability because the image dominates.
- Thobeck's product on a colored field, Cultivated Wit's logo/down cue and Squarespace's image/copy sections show varying content depth (pp. 21–23). Static arrows do not prove obvious or working navigation.
- Two Dots' phone/game promotion and Over Clothing's grid with a revealed View Details treatment illustrate different feature and product contexts (pp. 25–26). The screenshot cannot verify hover/touch/keyboard alternatives. A promotional website is not the game interface itself.
- Howard Yount's shoe hero, Ezorzi's faint texture, BBB's red field, Oak Street's boot product view, The Afrix's asymmetric sparse page and Wildflower's names/heart prompt offer visual alternatives (pp. 28–33). No animation or name/heart interaction was exercised.
- Resources pp. 34–35 and the p. 36 vendor back cover are historical pointers/offers; neither a palette tool nor a minimal theme is proof of accessibility or performance.

## Application to both modes

For the game marketing page, keep the story, gameplay, release/platform information and understandable primary action; remove decoration only if it does not help them. For the chatbot, remove redundant containers while retaining conversation navigation, clear message boundaries, progress/errors/retry and a usable composer. These are design-pro applications. Required labels and recovery remain visible even when a sparse screenshot looks cleaner without them.
