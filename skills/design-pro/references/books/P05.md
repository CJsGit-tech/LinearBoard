Exact filename: `Wireframes, Mockups, Prototypes/The Practical Handbook To Rapid Lo-Fi Prototyping.pdf`. Full text pp. 1–52 read and all 52 original pages inspected. Image-only p. 2 repeats the cover title/subtitle; it is not blank. Body folios match PDF positions. Linked previews, historical tutorials and services were not executed; no account or trial created.

## What a rough prototype can establish

Pp. 6–15 use the Katabatic expedition tent narrative, an anonymous radiology-portal anecdote, a build/review/refine loop and basic flow testing. This substantially repeats [P02](P02.md), including the tent/healthcare stories and ten low-fi versus two high-fi productivity comparison. Repetition is not independent outcome evidence. Original mountain and X-ray photographs do not document the claimed tests. The questions p. 12 explicitly are speculation, not the expedition's recorded protocol.

Retain the distinction between a static depiction and an operable sequence (p. 8), and the explicit limitation that quick feedback differs from in-context testing (p. 10). A rough prototype can expose route, ordering and interaction problems for a defined task; it cannot validate every aspect of a final product. Prototype, MVP and shipped implementation remain distinct despite the source's progression language (pp. 9, 11, 51). Anonymous healthcare feedback is not evidence of regulatory compliance or patient safety, and a tent analogy does not transfer physical safety results to software.

Reducing irrelevant detail may help discussion; visual simplicity does not guarantee unbiased feedback or realistic operation. Ten-versus-two is illustrative, not measured speed. Comparing variants is not automatically a valid A/B experiment (p. 14). The evidence claim must match participants, task, method, medium and actual observations.

## Model task, entry, content and state

Pp. 16–26 connect user goals, business goals, entry points and routes. The water/golf/airflow material is an analogy, not a quantified UX model or evidence that fuel savings reach ticket buyers (pp. 16–18). The report creator and consumer p. 18 may need different paths, but not necessarily disjoint apps or mandatory separate screens. User goals can be explicit without inventing a persona or treating a fictional profile as research (p. 19).

Search, direct, ads, social, referral and email are possible entry channels (pp. 19–21), not mandatory integrations. The Amazon paths are explicitly hypothetical, and the source qualifies the binary direct-versus-organic distinction. Preserve relevant entry state, prior knowledge and destination rather than inferring behavior solely from acquisition channel. Include deep links and returning to an interrupted task where relevant.

Pp. 22–24 show a writing-first outline and a shorthand of visible content above an action. In the original p. 23 diagram, Select Deposit Frequency → click Once per month → Select Calendar date → click Date maps content/action transitions. The four-step auto-deposit example is intentionally incomplete: it lacks other-frequency branches, account/source selection, review/confirmation, cancellation, validation, failures and final state. Do not treat it as a safe or complete financial workflow or turn every numbered action into a separate page. **Synthesis:** record entry condition → meaningful action → resulting state → recovery; group screens according to the task and information needed.

The phone sketches p. 24 and annotated signup outline p. 25 support route discussion. The latter includes product-team comments and reference screens; these do not prove actual user observations. Onboarding should help the user reach value (pp. 25–26); an upgrade funnel is optional and cannot justify obscuring choices. The Numbers welcome screenshot is a historical example, not a required tour.

## Choose a medium for the uncertainty

Pp. 27–39 compare paper, slides, code and specialized tools, overlapping [P04](P04.md). Paper can support quick exploration/manual state changes but cannot reproduce actual keyboard, rendering, timing or accessibility behavior. Twenty minutes versus two hours is a rhetorical comparison, not a general cost ratio. Art supplies do not guarantee inclusive collaboration; a hallway sample of three–five (p. 30), and the later minimum of five (p. 42), are not universal validation criteria. Choose participants and scope by the question and risk.

Context and task clarity matter for every medium. Do not adopt p. 29's blanket claim that valid research only uses spontaneous reactions without thought or that paper can yield no useful feedback. Distinguish conceptual feedback, facilitated operation and actual product behavior.

Slide links and reusable content can demonstrate paths (pp. 31–33); existing familiarity and a linear presentation do not guarantee complete nonlinear journeys. Claims about tool collaboration, product rankings, Invision's two-interaction limit, free trials and exports are historical and unverified-current. Specialized tools do not offer a perfect balance or guarantee better/faster work (pp. 36–39). Cloud storage and link sharing are not required for local collaboration or device equivalence.

Code may expose actual constraints and support reuse (pp. 34–36), but it is not automatically production-ready, modular or supported on every OS without software. A web prototype requires a compatible browser and does not establish native platform behavior. Do not select Rails, Python or HTML solely because the source names them. Match the existing project/environment and smallest useful runnable representation. Thirty minutes is not a benchmark; early code can either aid or constrain exploration depending on the task.

## Process and the tab invariant

Pp. 40–44 contrast a sequential sketch/wireframe/mockup/prototype/development process with earlier interaction or code. Retain the option to combine stages, test the chosen question and involve implementation concerns early. Fewer documents do not inherently mean better communication or quality; preserve necessary decisions/conditions that the prototype cannot convey. The recurring Yelp exercise p. 42 is acknowledged to be incomplete (p. 43); a depicted route does not establish production search or every breakpoint. No fixed low→high→code ladder is required.

Pp. 45–50 teach a historical multistate tab group and a matching multistate content group. Original p. 46 shows six labeled feature tabs and content, p. 47 selected tab states, p. 48 content grouping, and p. 49 two click actions. The invariant is that selected tab, displayed text and image refer to the same feature, while other tabs are deselected. Six whole group copies and two independent actions are a tool technique, not the desired architecture. A shared selected-item state may provide a simpler implementation. Original p. 50's selected Mockup tab and Mockup headline agree, but that static result still does not prove event wiring.

The tutorial omits keyboard/focus, semantic relationships, repeated selections, unexpected transitions and possible loading/errors. Add these when relevant to an implemented component; do not claim the screenshot proves accessibility. A breakpoint option in the editor also does not prove responsive rendering. Pp. 51–52 close with iteration and vendor promotion. The demonstrations should state simulated behavior and use actual browser checks for implemented navigation, content/state agreement and recovery, with limits reported separately from this source review.
