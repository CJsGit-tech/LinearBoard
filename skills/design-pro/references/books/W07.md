# W07 — Top 10 Web Design Trends of 2015

**Source:** UXPin, 2015; subtitle *A Lightweight Practical Guide*; Ben Gremillion and Jerry Cao (p. 4). Exact filename: `Web Design/Top 10 Web Design Trends of 2015.pdf`. PDF text pp. 1–28 read; originals pp. 1–2, 4–28 inspected. Body folios match PDF pages. A short historical selection, overlapping W03/W05 and related UI trend books, not a current ranking or independent effectiveness study. Static examples were inspected; no site, animation, vendor trial or usability result was executed.

## Start with the task, then select the technique

The introduction and conclusion advise starting with users rather than adopting a trend because it is fashionable (pp. 5, 27). Keep that conditional judgment. The conclusion's categorical claim that e-commerce cannot work with a single infinite-scrolling page is too broad; product discovery, navigation, comparison and checkout requirements must determine the actual structure. Finite long pages, feeds and scroll-driven animation are separate choices.

## Ten candidate techniques and their limits

1. **Interaction** (pp. 6–8): Beatbox visibly labels drums, kit change and navigation keys. Scenarios and role-playing can reveal gaps in a proposed interface conversation, but role-play is a simulation, not participant research. Keep clear action signifiers and relevant feedback. Faster completion is not every task's sole objective. Do not add location, notifications, sound or motion by default, require jQuery, or make Disney's twelve animation principles compulsory for every interface.
2. **Flat 2.0** (pp. 9–10): Toyota's illustrated car has a clear Start control against a subdued scene. Informal hybrid styling may retain depth cues without heavy ornament. A flat-looking graphic does not establish a smaller payload, easy comprehension or responsive behavior. An outlined ghost button still needs recognizable purpose and adequate contrast over the actual background.
3. **Long scrolling** (pp. 11–12): Shadow's original stacks a hero and two phone/message feature sections. A coherent narrative may suit a finite explanation; anchors or persistent access can help return to known content. Mobile traffic does not mandate length, decorative parallax or automatically extended feeds. The SEO assertions/linked advice are historical and were not adopted as current search guidance.
4. **Cards** (pp. 13–14): Behance's screenshot combines a multi-attribute grid with separate filtering controls. Group a coherent subject and distinguish units through space or meaningful styling. Do not assume everyone recognizes the whole surface as a link. Making the entire card activate one destination can conflict with secondary controls, selection or nested links; define those behaviors explicitly. No obligatory border, hover flip, spin or novel animation follows, and cards do not adapt effortlessly without implementation.
5. **Animation** (pp. 15–16): Bose's diagonally colored headphone arrangement illustrates a product composition; its movement is not visible in the static source. Motion can explain a state relationship, but is not inherently expected for usability. A spinner shows activity, not realistic remaining duration. Avoid invented progress, unnecessary waits and scroll effects that obstruct reading. See [current motion criteria](../current-accessibility.md#moving-and-auto-updating-content).
6. **Typography** (pp. 17–18): Google Ideas pairs a concise large statement and highlighted words with quieter navigation/details. Keep readable text and hierarchy. Two fonts is a starting heuristic, not a limit; bold sans-serif does not universally improve reading. Extreme small type still has to serve users. Distinguish typography as imagery from meaningful body copy and controls.
7. **Color** (pp. 19–20): Spotify's yellow/pink portrait and purple title illustrate a strong palette. Context/culture affect interpretation, and a monochromatic palette can still need several functional roles. A hover color change alone does not make a control discoverable before hover or usable through other inputs. Three palette schemes do not guarantee contrast. The historical web-safe-color discussion does not establish that high pixel density creates more color capability; avoid universal emotion claims.
8. **Minimalism** (pp. 21–22): OMEGA's original includes Menu, identity, a language/globe control, watch imagery and overlaid title. Reducing irrelevant competition may help; sparse UI does not prove fast loading, instant comprehension or sophistication. A concealed menu trades space for discovery effort. Keep access to required information and controls rather than prioritizing first impression over navigation.
9. **Photography** (pp. 23–24): Mario Frigerio's silhouette and overlaid destinations repeat the earlier gallery example. Choose imagery for meaningful product/use context, not guaranteed emotional/sales effects. An orange overlay does not automatically make a dismal scene friendly. Combining stock/custom imagery does not make it behavioral personalization or justify misleading provenance. Named stock services and licensing were not verified as current offerings.
10. **Background media** (pp. 25–26): R/m Design School combines a purple splash with typography/content panels. Protect legibility and actual task actions from background competition; preserve important subjects when adapting crops. Do not use the source's >200ppi HD/72ppi SD statement as a universal definition or infer adequate rendering from image metadata alone. See [W05's primary pixel-ratio clarification](W05.md#responsive-decisions-and-technical-corrections). SVG is suitable for some graphics, not every image; cropping is not compulsory solely because a camera supposedly has a usual aspect ratio. Evaluate useful detail, display size and resource cost together.

## Application and verification

For the Web game page, a distinctive visual identity can come from purposeful art/type while release information and the primary action remain clear without decorative motion. For mobile-web chat, grouped turns, a legible composer and accurate progress/error feedback matter more than trend ornament. These are design-pro applications. Evaluate actual input, focus, reflow, content extremes and media alternatives rather than treating the book's ten screenshots as evidence of accessibility or conversion. Pp. 27–28 end with vendor promotion, not current tool requirements.
