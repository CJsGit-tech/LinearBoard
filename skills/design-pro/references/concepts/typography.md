# Typography

Use type to make the reading order and message clear, while giving expressive brand moments an appropriate role.

## DO

- Test real text, the actual script, font fallback and viewport rather than assuming a family category guarantees readability. [T01, pp. 6–11, 24–26](../books/T01.md)
- Keep a stable reading edge for longer text; use centered treatment selectively for short headings or actions. The source illustrates left-to-right text; adapt to the language. [T01, pp. 15–17](../books/T01.md#decisions-worth-retaining)
- Inspect text and image crops together and keep meaningful subjects visible. [T02, pp. 16–18](../books/T02.md#practical-techniques)
- Document type by role with rendered examples and relevant variants. [S02, pp. 29–30](../books/S02.md)

## DON'T

- Do not turn 40 characters per line, 1.5 leading, two visual styles or oversized 85-point text into universal requirements. Those are contextual source heuristics or trend descriptions. [T01](../books/T01.md#measurements-and-exceptions); [T02](../books/T02.md#exceptions-and-evidence-limits)
- Do not remove semantic headings just to simplify a visual scale.
- Do not use motion to rescue unreadable necessary text.
- Do not reduce contrast because a historical screenshot uses pale gray.

The last three cautions are design-pro synthesis; current web checks are in [accessibility sources](../current-accessibility.md).

## Guidelines and example

A game title can use an expressive display treatment while its date/platform details use a restrained readable style. A chat transcript needs reading rhythm, long-message wrapping and legible status text. Change the visual role, not the meaning, across device widths. Check contrast and reflow on the actual result.

## Tone, hierarchy and actual text

Choose treatment by the message's role and reading context. A title may be expressive while a necessary error remains direct and legible; visual tone cannot replace precise meaning. Imagined persona reactions or age stereotypes are not user evidence. [U11, pp. 46–56](../books/U11.md#typography-supports-meaning-it-cannot-replace-it)

Inspect actual foreground/background combinations rather than choosing white on every gray surface. The source's 60–70 characters and 1.3–1.5 line-height advice is contextual Latin-text guidance, not a universal or accessibility requirement. No interface needs three hierarchy levels simply to meet a quota. [U11, pp. 57–69, including source contradictions](../books/U11.md#hierarchy-by-role-rather-than-a-fixed-quota)
