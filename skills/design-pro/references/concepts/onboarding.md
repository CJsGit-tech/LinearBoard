# Onboarding and first useful action

Onboarding helps a person complete their first meaningful task. It may explain controls, provide a trial action, collect necessary setup or combine these selectively. [X14, pp. 148–154](../books/X14.md#empowerment-recovery-and-onboarding)

## DO

- Identify what someone must know or supply before the first useful action.
- Place concise help near the relevant task and make optional guidance dismissible/revisitable when useful.
- Show actual setup progress if there are meaningful steps; preserve input when interruption/recovery matters.
- Use direct, respectful labels and explain what happens next. These applications follow the source's focus on core functionality and concise guidance. [X14, pp. 137–141, 148–154](../books/X14.md)

## DON'T

- Do not force a tour or account/profile questionnaire because another product used one.
- Do not imply optional personal data is required to reach “100%.”
- Do not use constant praise, playful copy or unexplained images where clear instructions are needed.
- Do not apply 80/20 as measured coverage or assume a hybrid tutorial always works best. [X14, pp. 149–153](../books/X14.md)

## Guidelines and example

Choose instruction for unfamiliar/high-consequence operations; choose direct use with contextual support when the action is understandable and reversible. Test what people can do, not merely whether they complete a slideshow. The decision rule is design-pro synthesis; no fixed number of screens or mandatory tour is established by the reviewed source.

For a sample chatbot, show an empty conversation with a clear composer and optional starter prompts. Identify simulated replies plainly where needed for honest product expectations. Keep retry and navigation discoverable during use rather than explaining every state in a preflight tour. Judge first-message completion and recovery separately from whether a visitor likes the welcome screen. [X17, pp. 6–8, 15](../books/X17.md#usability-prototypes-and-comparison)
