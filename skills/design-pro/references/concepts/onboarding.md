# Onboarding and first useful action

Onboarding helps a person complete their first meaningful task. It can explain controls, offer a trial action, collect necessary setup, or combine these. [X14, pp. 148–154](../books/X14.md#empowerment-recovery-and-onboarding)

## DO

[X14, pp. 137–141, 148–154](../books/X14.md)

- Identify what someone must know or supply before the first useful action.
- Place concise help beside the relevant task, and make optional guidance dismissible and revisitable.
- Show real setup progress when there are meaningful steps, and preserve input across interruption.
- Use direct, respectful labels and say what happens next.

## DON'T

- Do not force a tour or an account/profile questionnaire because another product has one.
- Do not present optional personal data as required to reach "100%".
- Do not use constant praise, playful copy or unexplained images where clear instructions are needed.
- Do not assume a hybrid tutorial works best or cite 80/20 as measured coverage; test this product's first task. [X14, pp. 149–153](../books/X14.md)

## Guidelines and example

Instruct up front for unfamiliar or high-consequence operations. For an understandable, reversible action, let people use it directly with contextual support. Test what people can do afterwards *(synthesis)*.

For a sample chatbot, show an empty conversation with a clear composer and optional starter prompts, and label simulated replies. Keep retry and navigation discoverable during use. Judge first-message completion and recovery separately from liking the welcome screen. [X17, pp. 6–8, 15](../books/X17.md#usability-prototypes-and-comparison)
