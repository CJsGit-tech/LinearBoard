# Information architecture and task flows

Organize content so people can predict where to find what supports their task. Design three things separately: content relationships, interface navigation and the sequence of actions. [X10, pp. 60–71](../books/X10.md#information-architecture-and-findability); [X06, pp. 13–19](../books/X06.md)

## DO

- Start with the goal and actual content, then choose categories and metadata from evidence about the audience. [X10, pp. 61–64](../books/X10.md)
- Use recognizable, honest labels and show representative deeper content where it sets expectations. [X10, pp. 62–64](../books/X10.md)
- Map a task's choices, transitions, result and recovery before polishing isolated screens. [X10, pp. 29–31](../books/X10.md#usability-task-structure-and-recovery)
- Use a story for action and outcome, a scenario for circumstances and a flow for paths. Keep research evidence apart from fictional examples. [X06, pp. 13–15](../books/X06.md#link-people-situations-and-paths)
- Plan who maintains content and terms; a sitemap, a taxonomy and a content repository meet different needs. [X06, pp. 16–19](../books/X06.md#plan-for-content-maintenance-early)

## DON'T

- Do not organize a product by the company's departments or a fashionable template *(synthesis)*.
- Do not impose a three-click limit; a longer set of understandable steps can serve the task better. [X10, p. 24](../books/X10.md)
- Do not produce a sitemap, persona or separate screen for a narrow component request; choose the artifact that helps. [X06, pp. 18–23](../books/X06.md)
- Do not defer a necessary mobile capability to a desktop-only step, or use categories the content does not fill. [X09, pp. 60–62](../books/X09.md#mobile-web-qualifications)

A format label is fine when visitors look for that format, such as a gameplay video *(synthesis; corrects [X10, pp. 63–64](../books/X10.md))*.

## Guidelines and example

Use open card sorting to explore groupings, closed sorting to examine given categories, tree testing to investigate label paths and click testing to examine a visible starting choice. Follow with the actual task flow where needed. [X10, pp. 70–71](../books/X10.md)

A game-release page may need one page with sections for identity, gameplay, release/platform details and the main action. Chat may need a conversation list, active transcript and composition/recovery states, with responsive presentations sharing one underlying task *(synthesis; demo assumptions to test)*.
