# Content and scanning

Develop meaningful draft content alongside the layout so its structure, length and meaning inform the design. Final copy is not a prerequisite for every sketch. [U11, pp. 6–18](../books/U11.md#develop-meaningful-content-alongside-the-layout)

## DO

- Use original, relevant sample text when real content is unavailable and identify fictional facts. Include long titles, date ranges and other meaningful edge cases.
- Reuse suitable authorized project content; inspect whether it still meets the task rather than assuming it will stay unchanged.
- Make headings, labels and links informative; put information needed to understand or act where it can be found.
- Evaluate the actual task and language at relevant widths. Inspect content and interaction together, including nonvisual access.

The first two apply [U11, pp. 13–18](../books/U11.md); the last two combine its content guidance with [the primary scanning clarification](../books/U11.md#primary-research-cross-check).

## DON'T

- Do not fit important meaning into arbitrary placeholder boxes by deleting necessary information.
- Do not copy a competitor's prose and rename it as original product content.
- Do not require an F/Z route, fixed corner for every CTA, or disruption every 1,000px. The book's assertions do not establish those as standards or universal outcomes. [U11, pp. 19–45](../books/U11.md#f-and-z-are-not-compulsory-routes-through-a-page)
- Do not treat editorial arrows on screenshots as eye-tracking evidence or promise a conversion increase.

## Guidelines and example

F-shaped scanning is an observed content-reading pattern under some conditions. It is one of several patterns; a clear hierarchy can help people find relevant information rather than letting unformatted text determine what is missed. It does not prescribe the entire page's navigation or identical words seen across reflowed layouts. This is external primary research interpretation, not a platform requirement. [NN/G cross-check, verified 2026-10-25](../books/U11.md#primary-research-cross-check)

Use task completion, ability to find necessary facts, misunderstandings and observed recovery to assess a layout. Do not substitute a presumed gaze path for those checks. These measures are design-pro synthesis; measurement requires an actual evaluation.

For a game release page, a useful sequence may answer what the game is, what play involves, when/where it releases and what the primary action does. For a chatbot, headings and message grouping support returning to the right conversation; a decorative zigzag is unnecessary. These are examples to evaluate, not fixed templates.
