# Content and scanning

Develop meaningful draft content alongside the layout so its structure, length and meaning inform the design. A sketch can start before final copy exists. [U11, pp. 6–18](../books/U11.md#develop-meaningful-content-alongside-the-layout)

## DO

- Use original, relevant sample text when real content is unavailable, and label fictional facts. Include long titles, date ranges and other edge cases. [U11, pp. 13–18](../books/U11.md)
- Reuse authorized project content and check it still meets the task. [U11, pp. 13–18](../books/U11.md)
- Make headings, labels and links informative; put information needed to understand or act where it will be found.
- Evaluate the actual task and language at the relevant widths. Inspect content and interaction together, including nonvisual access.

The last two combine U11's content guidance with [the primary scanning clarification](../books/U11.md#primary-research-cross-check).

## DON'T

- Do not delete necessary information to fit a placeholder box; resize the box.
- Do not copy a competitor's prose as product content.
- Do not require an F/Z route, a fixed corner for every CTA or a disruption every 1,000px; place content by the task. [U11, pp. 19–45](../books/U11.md#f-and-z-are-not-compulsory-routes-through-a-page)
- Do not present editorial arrows on a screenshot as eye-tracking evidence.

## Guidelines and example

F-shaped scanning is one reading pattern observed under some conditions. Respond to it with a clear hierarchy so people find what is relevant. [NN/G cross-check, verified 2026-10-25](../books/U11.md#primary-research-cross-check)

Assess a layout by task completion, ability to find necessary facts, misunderstandings and observed recovery *(synthesis)*.

For a game release page, a useful sequence answers what the game is, what play involves, when and where it releases, and what the primary action does. For a chatbot, headings and message grouping help people return to the right conversation *(synthesis)*.
