# Cards and grouping

A card presents a coherent content unit or decision. It can be useful for independent summaries, discovery or context-preserving detail; it is not a mandatory skin for every section. [M03, pp. 8–10, 16–22](../books/M03.md)

## DO

- Decide what the unit means and its primary interaction before drawing its surface.
- Use proximity to associate an author, message and relevant metadata.
- Distinguish compact summaries from expanded detail when the task needs both.
- Maintain readable content and usable activation regions as the layout reflows.

These recommendations apply [M03, pp. 8–10, 19, 21–27](../books/M03.md#principles-and-applications).

## DON'T

- Do not wrap every paragraph or list row in nested borders.
- Do not assume a container makes a gesture discoverable or an arbitrary layout responsive.
- Do not disguise advertising as ordinary content or make dismissal hard to activate. The source describes this favorably; design-pro deliberately rejects that framing. [M03, pp. 12–13](../books/M03.md#exceptions-and-source-critique)

## Guidelines and example

If the task is comparing the same attributes across many records, assess a table or aligned list. If the task is selecting among independent visual stories, cards may fit. This is design-pro synthesis, not a controlled result from the book.

For chat, group each turn coherently without an outer card around every avatar, message bubble and timestamp. For the release page, use a container only when it helps identify an independently meaningful item.

## Organization and adaptation

A card can contain several related fields while representing one coherent subject. Set its hierarchy and density by the task; a comparison may need aligned rows, while independent stories may benefit from summaries. Reflow and usable controls require implementation, not merely rectangular surfaces. [U12, pp. 6–20, 26–27](../books/U12.md)

Card sorting investigates content grouping and labels. It neither mandates card-shaped UI nor proves usability from a designer's own sort or an arbitrary five-person quota. [U12, p. 13](../books/U12.md#organization-and-responsive-behavior-require-actual-decisions)

Do not force images into 50–75% of every card, add novelty animation, or hide required actions behind hover. A text-only unit can be appropriate. Preserve meaningful reading/focus order when changing a grid; decide whether secondary controls, imagery and external sharing are actually useful. [U12, pp. 19–24](../books/U12.md#retained-practices-and-limits)

## Validate the content unit and its states

A card can combine multiple related fields and actions; it need not contain one isolated datum. Use realistic title lengths, metadata and state variations when assessing a grid. A screenshot or a 200px tutorial square does not establish usable targets, responsive behavior or correct overlay focus. Required controls need non-hover access. [W03, PDF pp. 45–72](../books/W03.md#minimal-appearance-and-cards-are-choices)

An expanded card link must coexist sensibly with secondary controls, text selection and keyboard navigation. Do not nest conflicting actions or force the entire surface to one destination merely to enlarge its target. Borders and novelty hover animation are optional. [W07, PDF pp. 13–14](../books/W07.md#ten-candidate-techniques-and-their-limits); these safeguards qualify the source's whole-card prescription.
