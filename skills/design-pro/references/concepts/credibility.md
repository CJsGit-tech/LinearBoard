# Credibility, reassurance and truthful presentation

Credibility starts with accurate information and behavior that fulfills its promise. Visual order can help comprehension but cannot establish security, reliability or a real customer relationship. [X09, p. 23](../books/X09.md#ux-usefulness-and-evidence); [X10, pp. 87–105](../books/X10.md#credibility-and-truthful-evidence)

## DO

- Label the action and deliver the expected result; distinguish pending, failed and completed operations. [X10, pp. 25–26, 93–94](../books/X10.md)
- State actual availability, costs, delivery expectations and material conditions in the relevant context. [X10, pp. 102–104](../books/X10.md)
- Use real, correctly attributed testimonials/ratings/results if they exist; include definitions, time and limitations where numbers need context. [X10, pp. 95–101 and qualifications](../books/X10.md#credibility-and-truthful-evidence)
- Explain simulated services and fictional content clearly in demos; keep design rationale and citations in companion documents. This is design-pro application of the source's truthfulness principle.

## DON'T

- Do not fabricate reviews, users, endorsements, security badges or release availability to make a page feel finished.
- Do not equate a lock icon, blue palette or rounded form with actual protection. [X08, p. 9](../books/X08.md#communicate-the-offering-and-the-operation); [X10, pp. 89–90, 103](../books/X10.md)
- Do not show fake Save/Refresh actions or artificial waiting to manufacture confidence or imply computation. Design-pro rejects these source recommendations. [X07, pp. 11–17](../books/X07.md#deliberate-rejection-of-deceptive-controls)
- Do not conceal material terms or obstruct cancellation to improve a narrow conversion measure. [X10, pp. 93–94](../books/X10.md)

## Guidelines and example

Separate perceived credibility from verified capability. Source anecdotes about attractive interfaces and testimonials are not causal proof of trust or business success. When no genuine social proof exists, improve clarity and show the actual product instead of inventing a testimonial strip. [X10, pp. 88–101](../books/X10.md)

For a fictional game, use original sample release details and gameplay descriptions, identify it as a concept demo and make a signup confirmation say it is a local simulation. For a chatbot, retry should act on a real failed simulated response state rather than pretending a live AI service was contacted. These are design-pro applications.

## Appearance evidence has a limited scope

A frequently repeated Stanford figure is about the proportion of coded comments mentioning design look, not the proportion of all users trusting a site on looks alone. Do not use it as proof that polish establishes security/truth or a guaranteed business effect. [U17, p. 10 and focused primary-report cross-check, verified 2026-10-25](../books/U17.md#credibility-claim-checked-against-its-primary-source)

Assess perceived clarity separately from actual task completion, accuracy and reliability. Rounded rectangles, a familiar logo position, color mood or a human photo are contextual choices; none proves the service fulfills its promise. [U17, pp. 10–21, 27–38](../books/U17.md)
