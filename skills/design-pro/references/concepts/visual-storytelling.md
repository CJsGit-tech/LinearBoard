# Visual storytelling and optional interaction

Use visual narrative to explain something the audience needs to understand. Add interactivity only where the task benefits. [U01, pp. 6–7, 26](../books/U01.md#choose-interaction-only-when-it-serves-the-story-and-task)

## DO

[U01, pp. 15–26](../books/U01.md)

- Connect imagery, copy and sequence to the audience's goal and the product's role in that journey.
- Use a single image or a clear static sequence when it communicates enough.
- Keep critical information and actions visibly discoverable; reserve easter eggs for optional exploration.
- Use feedback to clarify an actual action or state change.

## DON'T

[U01, pp. 7, 17–23](../books/U01.md)

- Do not make release information or the primary action depend on finding a hidden interaction.
- Do not force a long story on a quick information task.
- Do not use hover-only reveals for required content, or leave the page obscured when decorative motion is off.
- Do not offer a static screenshot as verification of scrolling, animation or input access; exercise them.

## Guidelines and example

Start with what must be understood, then choose the sequence and representation. A narrative can cover the site's part in a longer journey. Beginning/middle/end is one planning option. [U01, pp. 24–25](../books/U01.md#map-the-wider-journey-without-inventing-a-universal-story-arc)

For a game-release page, a concise world/gameplay introduction leads to platform/release information and a clear next action, with atmospheric artwork kept clear of navigation, reading and the action. For chat, readable messages and honest feedback are usually enough *(synthesis)*. Check [current motion/auto-update criteria](../current-accessibility.md) and test input and motion alternatives in the implementation.

## Choose meaningful imagery without effect-size promises

Choose a photo, diagram, illustration or mascot for what it explains. Give a familiar icon an understandable purpose in context. [U08, PDF pp. 7–35, 51–67](../books/U08.md)

Provide [text alternatives](../current-accessibility.md#non-text-content), keep the information available without decorative effects, and make audio opt-in. Carry a video's meaningful information in text as well as its poster image. [U08, PDF pp. 36–50](../books/U08.md#medium-and-visual-style-follow-the-job); [current audio guidance](../current-accessibility.md#audio-control)

## Choose the scrolling model explicitly

A finite story, an extending feed and scroll-controlled animation solve different problems; pick one deliberately. Keep direct access to known information and the person's location. Show essential text without waiting for a scroll animation. [W03, PDF pp. 134–157](../books/W03.md#separate-scrolling-formats)
