# Distinctive design grounded in the product

Use in `create-designs` and `create-visuals` for a new identity, substantial composition work or feedback that results look generic. These methods are self-contained; no extra design skill, book-reading ritual or tool is required. A bounded correction retains the existing direction.

## Establish the brief, then form an idea

Read the vision (including intended player experience for games), grouped story entries, PRDs, protected interactions, platform and existing approved identity. Name the primary task, audience, real content/data density and desired experience. Separate binding constraints, user preferences and open creative choices. Do not turn an agent's earlier example into a constraint.

Find a visual idea in this product's subject, actions or tensions. Explain its connection in a sentence, then translate it into visible relationships: what dominates, how attention travels, which information stays together, image/type balance, density and quiet regions. A genre label such as 'modern premium', 'playful SaaS' or 'minimal dashboard' is not a concept. Familiar controls can remain familiar; distinction need not appear in every component.

For games distinguish the playfield, HUD, menus and overlays. The interface should support the intended player attention and game art, not recast gameplay as a business dashboard. Do not invent game rules or promise motion/feel from a still image.

## Inspect references with a purpose

Reuse user references and actual existing product screens first. When a new direction is needed and references are absent or uninformative, proactively find and visually inspect a small relevant set. Do not require the user to supply examples or choose a style name. Use primary product/designer/publisher pages when available, browse for new external research, and inspect the actual image/page rather than a search snippet. Record exact URLs and what was actually inspected in the owning LinearBoard design document; a link alone is not evidence of visual study. Report inaccessible references rather than inventing observations.

References may come from the product category, editorial work, physical objects, maps, signage, craft or the game's subject where relevant. No fixed reference count or mix is required. For each useful reference distinguish observed visual relationships, the principle being adapted, and what will be changed for this product. Transform composition methods; do not copy another product's branding, artwork, proprietary assets or complete screen. Reference access is not an asset license.

## Make a recommendation instead of another style questionnaire

The agent owns routine creative judgment. State the recommended idea, its concrete visual consequences and tradeoff. Compare alternatives only when uncertainty or the user request warrants it. Alternatives must solve the same brief and differ in structure or representation; recoloring a common hero/sidebar/card grid does not count. Ask only about missing consequential intent or changes to binding requirements. Explicitly delegated choices need no extra approval; an agent recommendation remains a proposal until its actual selection scope is established.

Carry the idea into a connected visual grammar: type roles/contrast, scale and spacing rhythm, color roles and area, imagery/art direction, grouping and important component states. Avoid a universal recipe for beige/serif, neon/glass, brutalism or any other look. Do not add animation, huge headings, asymmetry, gradients or ornament just to signal originality.

Prefer continuous grouping through alignment, spacing and typography where it suits the brief; panels, cards, borders and elevation need a meaningful grouping, interaction or state purpose. A data table, game inventory or native control may require strong boundaries. Keep discoverability, focus, readable text, contrast and accessible targets. This is a composition preference, not a blanket ban on components.

## Interaction belongs to the direction

Follow [interaction, loading and motion](interaction-loading-motion.md) for applicable behavior. Motion supports cause/effect and continuity; it is not a substitute for a product-specific idea. Judge hover, selection, focus and waiting feedback within the same visual grammar, with usable touch and reduced-motion alternatives. Do not add generic effects or force every state onto every component.

## Critique the actual result

Inspect the image itself, not just its prompt or caption. Use these questions to identify a visible correction:

- Does the first focal point support the primary task or intended player attention? Can essential content/actions still be found?
- What specific composition/type/imagery relationship expresses this product's idea? If the name and content were swapped for an unrelated product, what would remain distinctive? This is a diagnostic, not an originality test.
- Are alternatives visibly different at thumbnail scale, beyond palette and labels? Are repeated frames fragmenting the whole?
- Does the idea survive the actual content density, a long label and the required narrow/state variant? Do not invent edge-case rules merely to draw them.
- Are the chosen grammar and content consistent between images? Are labels readable and controls/source behavior faithful?

Identify the visible weakness, make a targeted revision and inspect it again. Reject unsupported content or unreadable critical information; beauty cannot compensate for a broken brief. Do not deliver several known weak concepts just to fill a quota. Make one focused repair pass per identified weakness; if the same weakness persists, narrow the composition or present the unresolved choice/limit rather than silently looping through expensive generations.

Record concise observed changes and material limits in the owning canonical document/comment. Do not invent scores, usability results, uniqueness guarantees or claims that loading these rules improves the model itself. Written guidance and still images cannot establish actual interaction, accessibility conformance, conversion or player enjoyment.
